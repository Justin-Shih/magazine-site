中文完整摘要說明

容器式衛生服務從利基創新逐漸成為城市與監管機構重視的實務選項，特別適合傳統下水道難以到達、空間有限、土壤困難或易淹水地區。文章指出 CBS 已在多國擴張，並透過使用者需求、成果型融資、碳權和公共系統整合，展現包容性與氣候韌性潛力。

﻿## 崛起中的容器式衛生服務 Isabella Montgomery 強調容器式衛生服務能提供的機會，包括促進包容性與氣候韌性。 容器式衛生服務（container-based sanitation，CBS）起初是一項利基創新，如今越來越受到公用事業、監管機構和部會認可，被視為擴大安全服務的一種實務方法，尤其適用於傳統系統無法觸及的地點。Container Based Sanitation Alliance（CBSA）2025 年部門回顧《Container-based sanitation, State of the Sector, 2025》捕捉到 CBS 正在出現且意義重大的轉變。其中包括透過碳權和成果型融資創造新收入，為提升氣候韌性並滿足成長中城市的未被滿足需求提供途徑。 CBS 能協助城市把「不讓任何人掉隊」的目標化為現實，因為它能為邊緣社群和脆弱人口提供衛生服務。CBS 的核心，是使用能把排泄物收集在小型、可密封、可移除容器中的廁所，這些容器會被定期收集並在場外安全處理。這種方法非常適合難以到達的地區，包括空間受限、土壤堅硬或多岩石、或容易淹水的地區，因此特別適合密集都市聚落和非正式社區。 研究顯示，與典型衛生選項相比，CBS 也能大幅降低溫室氣體排放，最高可減少 93%。同時，它的設計與適應性服務模式，也使其能在多重氣候壓力下保持韌性。 CBS 的擴張速度比許多人想像得快。2024 年，超過 43.2 萬人取得 CBS 服務，六年間增加六倍，平均年成長率約 35%。相比之下，撒哈拉以南非洲的安全管理衛生服務每年僅成長 2%；常被拿來類比的行動支付和 pay-as-you-go 太陽能，年成長率分別為 19% 和 16%。這條成長軌跡令人鼓舞，即使要縮小整體衛生缺口，仍迫切需要更快的採用速度。 這個部門的成長也伴隨多樣化。雖然少數服務提供者最廣為人知，但目前已有約 25 家機構在至少 18 個國家提供 CBS 服務和解方，類型從市政機關、合作社，到社會企業、非政府組織和私人公司都有。CBS 服務也出現在意想不到的地方，包括高收入環境，例如英國倫敦 Circular Revolution 的運河船服務，以及美國加州 Santa Cruz 野火後的應變服務。這兩個案例都展現 CBS 在高度監管環境中提供安全衛生服務的適應能力。南非也出現數個新的服務提供者，當地衛生創新正蓬勃發展。 關鍵在於，這種擴張是由強勁使用者需求支撐的。跨多個情境的研究顯示，即使沖水廁所仍是長期願望，CBS 也能滿足人們對安全、尊嚴與便利的期待。 服務提供者持續投資於設計、使用者體驗和共同創造，確保廁所既令人願意使用，也能可靠運作。顯然，CBS 能提供立即且具體的效益，同時也指向未來更具企圖心且更有韌性的衛生可能性。這使 CBS 成為致力於包容性、氣候智慧型衛生服務的城市可以採用的實務路徑。 這個部門也已展現出有意義的城市尺度進展。過去，CBS 部門曾被質疑能否證明規模化能力，並發展出足以吸引政府持續關注的服務規模。然而，肯亞 Nairobi 的 Fresh Life 已回應這項挑戰，目前觸及該市非正式人口的 9%，以及安全管理非下水道家庭的 60%。在海地小鎮 Caracol，SOIL 這個在海地設計、測試並實施永續且公平衛生危機解方的非營利研發組織，服務超過 20% 的人口。這些案例顯示，CBS 能以高服務量運作，並提供有意義的都市覆蓋。 然而，真正的系統性規模化需要的不只是高使用人數。它要求 CBS 被嵌入公共系統、法規和長期融資安排之中。 ### 規模化與永續性的路徑 衛生是一項公共財，CBS 也不例外。令人鼓舞的是，CBS 部門正開始透過公共合作、新融資和制度認可，在系統整合方面取得重要進展。多國政府正在直接與 CBS 服務提供者合作，包括在海地資助 SOIL、在尚比亞與 Fresh Life 共同資助市政試點、在肯亞透過夥伴支持擴張，以及在秘魯與 Sanima 進行部會合作。這些安排協助城市擴大覆蓋、動員資源並展示營運可信度，也表明 CBS 已準備好被嵌入更廣泛的都市衛生策略。 ### CBS 的融資 融資仍然是關鍵推動因素。成果型融資將資金直接連結到經驗證的服務交付，提供一種近似公共採購、同時降低市政風險的機制。透過證明 CBS 服務提供者能符合合約標準，成果型融資為大規模公共採購鋪路。迄今為止，CBS 部門已在海地和肯亞動員 450 萬美元成果型融資，並帶動顯著成長。 氣候融資也提供另一項收入來源。CBS 的氣候效益使其能取得碳權，並且具備進一步取得其他資金流的有利位置。同時，CBS 能催化更長期行為改變的認知，也正在吸引開發銀行支持。SOIL 在海地的證據顯示，家庭往往會轉向永久衛生設施，促使 Inter-American Development Bank（IDB）等機構把 CBS 視為強化系統的模式，進一步擴大潛在融資來源。 對城市主管機關，以及延伸而言對監管者、資助者和部門規劃者，關鍵機會包括：擴大覆蓋並催化行為改變，因為 CBS 能鼓勵家庭投資永久衛生基礎設施，推動系統整體改善；透過成果型融資降低風險，因為服務提供者若能證明符合市政採購要求，就能為城市提供低風險的規模化路徑；提高營運效率，讓面臨服務交付最佳化壓力的公用事業，能運用服務提供者創新來釋放新的效率和收入來源；採取氣候行動，因為 CBS 較低排放並可與碳市場整合，使主管機關能達成氣候與環境目標；以及支持高品質服務提供者，因為 CBS 規模化需要政策框架、公共招標和公私協調來支持營運者，確保服務保持安全、可靠且公平。 ### 前景 多年來，衛生部門一直受到「臨時」與「永久」解方之間狹隘區分的影響，即使基礎設施密集型方法交付緩慢或不切實際，仍往往被視為預設選項。CBS 近期發展反映了部門中已在進行的更廣泛轉變，也就是更重視系統思維、服務連續性，以及在複雜都市環境中實現包容性衛生的實務路徑。 CBS 服務提供者正在展示，主動管理的非下水道服務如何能在公用事業難以維持連續服務的密集低收入地區，擴大安全管理衛生覆蓋。這些轉變與當前部門優先事項相呼應，包括 citywide inclusive sanitation（CWIS）以及 IWA 在 inclusive urban sanitation（IUS）方面的工作；這些方向都正推動都市衛生規劃走向公平、韌性和財務永續的服務模式。在這樣的格局中，CBS 越來越被視為城市可用來縮小服務缺口並建立氣候韌性的組合之一。 CBS 的動能是真實的，但挑戰仍然存在。融資缺口、法規障礙和營運複雜性，如果未被策略性處理，都可能限制規模化。然而，城市及其夥伴若能透過政策支持、目標性融資，以及與服務提供者合作進行周延參與，就能把 CBS 的潛力轉化為對服務不足社區具有意義且持續的影響。 更多資訊：《Container-based sanitation, State of the Sector, 2025》可於 cbsa.global/state-of-the-sector 下載。 作者 Isabella Montgomery 是 Container Based Sanitation Alliance 的國際發展專案經理。

The Container Based Sanitation Alliance (CBSA) 2025 sector review, titled Container-based sanitation, State of the Sector, 2025, captures emerging but significant shifts in CBS. These include new revenue in the form of carbon credits and outcomes-based funding that are providing ways to improve climate resilience and fulfil unmet demand in growing cities. CBS can help cities make the aim of “leaving no one behind” a reality by providing sanitation to marginalised communities and vulnerable populations. At its core, CBS involves toilets that collect waste in small, sealable, removable containers, which are regularly collected and safely treated off-site ~-a method well suited to hard-to- reach areas, including those with space constraints, hard or rocky soils, or areas prone to flooding — making CBS particularly suitable for dense urban settlements and informal communities. Research has shown that CBS can also dramatically reduce greenhouse gas emissions — by up to 93% over typical sanitation options — while its design and adaptive service model make it resilient in the face of multiple climate stresses. 66 CBS has expanded faster than many realise CBS has expanded faster than many realise. In 2024, more than 432,000 people accessed CBS services — a six- fold increase over six years — averaging approximately 35% annual growth. By comparison, safely managed sanitation in Sub-Saharan Africa grew at just 2% per year, while sectors often cited as parallels, such as mobile money or pay-as-you-go solar, grew respectively 19% and 16% annually. This trajectory is encouraging, even if much faster uptake is urgently needed to close the overall sanitation gap. The sector’s growth is matched by diversity. While a handful of providers are most widely known, around 25 enterprises now deliver CBS services and solutions in at least 18 countries, ranging from municipal authorities and cooperatives to social enterprises, NGOs and private companies. Both of these examples demonstrate CBS's adaptability for providing safe sanitation in highly regulated environments. There are also several new providers in South Africa, where sanitation innovation is flourishing. Crucially, this expansion is underpinned by strong user demand. Research across multiple contexts shows that CBS meets expectations for safety, dignity and convenience, even when flush toilets remain the long-term aspiration. Providers are continuing to invest in design, user experience and co-creation, ensuring that toilets are both desirable and function reliably. It is evident that CBS can deliver immediate, tangible benefits, while pointing the way toward more aspirational and resilient sanitation possibilities in the future — positioning the approach as a practical pathway for cities committed to inclusive, climate- smart sanitation. The sector can also point to meaningful citywide traction. The sector has in the past been challenged to show proof of scale and to develop services large enough to draw sustained government attention. However, Fresh Life in Nairobi, Kenya, has risen to that challenge, now reaching 9% of the city’s informal population and 60% of safely managed non-sewered households. In the small town of Caracol, Haiti, SOIL (a non-profit research and development organisation working in Haiti to design, test, and implement sustainable and equitable solutions to the sanitation crisis) is serving more than 20% of the population. These examples show that CBS can. operate at high volumes and deliver meaningful urban coverage. However, true systemic scale requires more than high user numbers — it requires CBS to be embedded into public systems, regulations and long-term financing arrangements. Pathways to scale and sustainability Sanitation is a public good, and CBS is no exception. Encouragingly, the sector is starting to make important inroads in systemic integration through public collaboration, new funding and institutional recognition. Governments in multiple countries are engaging directly with CBS providers, whether through funding SOIL in Haiti, municipal pilot co-financing with Fresh Life in Zambia, partnership supported expansion in Kenya, or ministerial collaboration with Sanima, Peru. These arrangements. help cities extend coverage, mobilise resources and demonstrate operational credibility, signalling that CBS is ready to be embedded in broader urban sanitation strategies. Funding CBS Financing remains a critical enabler. Outcome-based financing, which links funding directly to verified service delivery, provides a mechanism that mirrors public procurement while mitigating risk for municipalities. By demonstrating that CBS providers can meet contractual standards, outcome-based financing paves the way for public contracting at scale. To date, the sector has mobilised $4.5 million in outcome-based financing in Haiti and Kenya and is triggering notable growth. Climate finance offers another revenue stream. The climate benefits of CBS have enabled it to secure carbon credits, and it is well positioned to access further funding streams. Evidence from SOIL in Haiti shows households often transition to permanent sanitation, prompting institutions such as the Inter-American Development Bank (IDB) to view CBS as a system-strengthening model — further widening the pool of potential finance. Key opportunities for city authorities — and by extension, regulators, funders, and sector planners — include opportunities to: @ Expand coverage and catalyse behaviour change: CBS can encourage households to invest in permanent sanitation infrastructure, helping to drive system-wide improvements. @ Reduce risk through outcome-based financing: By demonstrating that providers can meet municipal procurement requirements, CBS can offer cities a low-risk path to scale. @ Increase operational efficiency: Utilities under pressure to optimise service delivery can leverage provider innovations to unlock new efficiencies and revenue streams. @ Take climate action: CBS’s lower emissions and integration with carbon markets enables authorities to meet climate and environmental targets. @ Support high-quality providers: Scaling CBS requires operator support through policy frameworks, public tenders and public- private coordination to ensure services remain safe, reliable and equitable. The outlook For years, the sector has been shaped by a narrow distinction between ‘temporary’ and ‘permanent’ solutions, often defaulting to infrastructure-heavy approaches even when they were slow or impractical to deliver. Recent developments in CBS mirror the broader shifts already under way in the sector —a stronger focus on systems thinking, service continuity and practical routes to achieving inclusive sanitation in complex urban environments. CBS providers are demonstrating how actively managed, non-sewered services can extend safely managed coverage in dense, low- income areas where utilities struggle to maintain continuity.
