# 保障雪梨城市水源 A.S.M. Mohiuddin、Ben Blayney 與 Saravanamuthu Vigneswaran 說明，澳洲雪梨如何面對氣候變遷與極端天氣事件，以創新方式提升水安全。 雪梨的飲用水主要來自蓄存在水壩中的地表水，並由 Sydney Water 的水過濾廠（Water Filtration Plants, WFPs）處理。這些水廠多數採用直接過濾或接觸過濾程序。整體而言，這些水過濾廠每年為雪梨及周邊地區超過 560 萬名用戶生產並供應超過 6,000 億公升飲用水。 過去十年，水源集水區中的氣候事件變得更頻繁也更嚴重，包括乾旱、叢林大火與極端降雨。這些事件已顯著惡化雪梨各水壩的原水水質。快速變化的原水條件，讓水過濾廠營運承受前所未有的壓力，有時甚至挑戰城市飲用水供應的連續性。 2018 至 2020 年間，雪梨最重要水源 Warragamba Dam 發生了一連串關鍵事件。這座水壩供應雪梨約 80% 的用水需求，相當於約 400 萬人口的供水量。在 2018 至 2019 年乾旱期間，這座可蓄存 2,031 億公升水、相當於雪梨三年供水量的水壩，水位下降到嚴重偏低的水準。到了 2020 年 2 月，水壩水位降至約容量的 43%。 雪梨仍處於乾旱期間時，2019 年 12 月至 2020 年 1 月發生大型叢林大火，燒毀 Warragamba 集水區約 32 萬公頃土地，相當於該集水區 35%。大火後不久，2020 年 2 月 7 日至 10 日出現打破乾旱的強降雨，Warragamba 集水區記錄到約 400 至 500 毫米降雨。這場極端降雨使水壩從 2 月 8 日的 43% 蓄水量，也就是 870 GL，在 2 月 13 日回升到 74%，也就是 1,500 GL。換言之，短短五天內淨流入量約達 6,300 億公升。 這股突然逕流把大量灰燼、沉積物與有機物帶入水壩。因此，濁度被記錄為超過 100 NTU，真色度在 400 nm 下超過 50 CU，這些數值都超出水壩下游三座接觸過濾水廠的處理極限。從 2020 到 2024 年，多次極端降雨事件反覆出現，並進一步加劇這些水質衝擊。 在這段期間，原水中的真色度與溶解性有機碳（Dissolved Organic Carbon, DOC）濃度增加三到五倍，反映天然有機物（Natural Organic Matter, NOM）大幅上升。Sydney Water 的研究發現，DOC 中有 70% 至 80% 由疏水性腐植酸構成，而這類物質處理起來尤其困難。 原水中升高的腐植物質干擾了混凝程序，形成脆弱絮體，並在過濾過程中崩解。這使濾池運轉時間縮短，也讓水過濾廠產能下降到約設計容量的 50%。對雪梨飲用水可靠性而言，這是一項重大風險。 ## 韌性創新 自 2020 年極端天氣事件開始以來，Sydney Water 透過 Sydney Water Instantaneous Filtration Technology（SWIFT）發展出先進的數位最佳化能力。SWIFT 是一套動態製程模型，整合進即時的監督控制與資料擷取系統（SCADA）中。 傳統水處理最佳化仰賴瓶杯試驗來決定最佳化學藥劑投加量，接著再由水廠進行手動調整與試誤。這種方法耗時，通常每天只能做一到兩次投加量變更，無法跟上原水水質的快速變動。在洪水事件期間，等到水廠根據瓶杯試驗完成最佳化時，原水水質可能已經改變，導致營運難以維持；在某些情況下，甚至可能無法生產安全乾淨的飲用水。 SWIFT 模型從根本上改變了這個典範。模型背後建立在對極端天氣下原水條件惡化所造成濾池性能下降的進階研究之上，並辨識出絮體完整性不佳是根本原因。這對水產業而言是一項重要的新洞見。 SWIFT 模型把這項研究轉化為卓越營運能力。它建立絮體完整性與化學藥劑投加之間的新關聯，使系統能動態、即時預測最佳化學投加量，以提升過濾表現。 模型會吸收濾池每分鐘更新的即時營運資料，並套用預測演算法，即時判定最佳化學投加量。它也能在數分鐘內向水廠操作人員提供可採取行動的洞見，使操作人員能立即回應原水條件變化。SWIFT 的這項能力，是水處理最佳化的一項突破，因為它提供連續、預測式的調整，而不是緩慢、反應式且人工的控制。 ## SWIFT 的發展 SWIFT 演算法是使用 Sydney Water 各水過濾廠超過 12 年的營運資料開發而成。它首次部署於 2020 年極端原水水質事件期間，隨後逐步推展，並於 2024 年在所有水過濾廠全面導入。 自導入以來，SWIFT 已協助 Sydney Water 在三次重大「百年一遇」洪水中維持穩定水廠表現，確保雪梨超過 560 萬名用戶的安全乾淨飲用水供應不中斷。 透過既有水過濾廠的最佳化，SWIFT 模型降低了新前處理升級所需的容量，為未來基礎設施帶來顯著資本支出節省。SWIFT 模型所達成的先進水處理最佳化，結合有針對性的前處理升級，將有助於降低處理水中的 DOC 濃度，進而減少飲用水管網中消毒副產物的形成。 在數位最佳化倡議同步推進的同時，Sydney Water 也投入大量資本投資，以強化水處理系統面對愈來愈極端、愈來愈多變原水水質時的韌性。這項投資的核心，是位於 Prospect 水過濾廠的新大型前處理設施。Prospect 是雪梨最大的水過濾廠。 Prospect 前處理廠已開始施工，處理能力為每日 7.5 億公升。這座前處理廠採用高速澄清程序，將降低原水中的濁度、色度、藻類與有機物，進而改善下游水過濾廠的表現。這項升級將在過濾前移除更高負荷的沉積物、濁度、色度，以及 DOC 與 NOM，顯著提升原水澄清能力。 新設施是專門為管理乾旱、叢林大火後果與強降雨事件所伴隨的嚴重水質惡化而設計。透過穩定並改善水質，這項升級將保護過濾性能，並確保極端事件期間安全飲用水生產能持續進行。新的前處理設施預計於 2027 至 2028 年投入運轉。 其他 Sydney Water 關鍵水過濾廠也正在施工規劃中的前處理升級，這將進一步強化雪梨水處理韌性。所有目前的前處理升級預計於 2030 年完成。全面導入後，這些投資將建立一個強健、具氣候韌性的處理網絡，即使面對未來氣候不確定性，也能可靠滿足雪梨成長中人口的需求，並維持最高標準的飲用水安全。

Mohiuddin, Ben Blayney, and Saravanamuthu Vigneswaran describe how Sydney, Australia, is innovating to improve water security in the face of climate change and extreme weather events. ydney’s drinking water is sourced primarily from surface water stored in dams and treated at Sydney Water’s Water Filtration Plants (WEPs), most of which use direct or contact filtration processes. These WFPs collectively produce and supply more than 600 billion litres of drinking water every year to over 5.6 million customers across Sydney and surrounding regions. Over the past decade, increasingly frequent and severe climatic events in water catchments — droughts, bushfires, and extreme rainfall - have significantly degraded raw water quality across Sydney’s dams. These rapid changes have placed unprecedented stress on WFP operations and, at times, have challenged continuity of the city’s drinking water supply. A critical sequence of events occurred between 2018 and 2020 at Warragamba Dam, which is Sydney’s most important water source. It supplies around 80% of the city’s water demand for an equivalent population of approximately 4 million people. During the 2018-19 drought, the dam — which stores 2031 billion litres of water (equivalent to three years of supply for Sydney) — depleted to critically low storage levels. By February 2020 the dam level had dropped to about 43% of capacity. While Sydney remained in drought, there was a major bushfire between December 2019 and January 2020 that burned approximately 320,000 hectares (35%) of the Warragamba catchment. Shortly after the bushfires, intense drought-breaking rainfall occurred from 7-10 February 2020, with approximately 400-500 mm of rain recorded within the Warragamba catchment. This extreme rainfall refilled the dam from 43% (870 GL) on 8 February to 74% (1500 GL) on 13 February 2020 —a net inflow of approximately 630 billion litres within just five days. As a result, turbidity was recorded as above 100 Nephelometric Turbidity Units (NTU) and true colour above 50 colour units (CU) at 400 nm, which are beyond the treatment limits of the three contact filtration WFPs downstream of the dam. Multiple extreme rain events from 2020 to 2024 have since repeated and exacerbated these water quality impacts. During this period, true colour and Dissolved Organic Carbon (DOC) concentrations in raw water increased three- to five-fold, reflecting a substantial rise in Natural Organic Matter (NOM). Research by Sydney Water found that 70-80% of the DOC consisted of hydrophobic humic acids, which are particularly challenging to treat. Elevated humic substances in raw water have disrupted coagulation processes and produced weak flocs that disintegrated during filtration. This reduced filter run times and decreased the WFP’s output to around 50% of design capacity — representing a major risk for Sydney’s drinking water reliability. Aerial view of the Prospect Water Filtration Plant Innovation for resilience Since the onset of extreme weather events in 2020, Sydney Water has developed advanced digital optimisation through its Sydney Water Instantaneous Filtration Technology (SWIFT) - a dynamic process model integrated into real-time Supervisory Control and Data Acquisition (SCADA) systems. Traditional optimisation of water treatment relies on jar tests to determine optimum chemical dose rates, followed by manual adjustment and trial and error at a treatment plant. This approach is time consuming, often allowing only one or two dose changes per day and cannot keep pace with rapidly changing raw water quality. During flood events, by the time a plant is optimised based on a jar test, raw water quality may have already changed, leading to unsustainable operations and, in some cases, an inability to produce safe and clean drinking water. The SWIFT model fundamentally changes this paradigm. The model is underpinned by advanced research into deteriorated filter performance caused by degraded raw water conditions during extreme weather events, identifying poor floc integrity as the root cause ~ an important new insight for the water industry. The SWIFT model translates this research into operational excellence by establishing novel correlations between floc integrity and chemical dosing, enabling dynamic, real-time prediction of optimum chemical doses to enhance filtration performance. The model ingests live, minute-by-minute operational data of the filters and applies predictive algorithms to determine optimal chemical dosing in real-time. It also provides actionable insights to plant operators within minutes, enabling immediate response to changing raw water conditions. This capability in SWIFT represents a breakthrough in water treatment optimisation providing continuous, predictive adjustment rather than slow, reactive and manual control. SWIFT development The SWIFT algorithm was developed using more than 12 years of operational data across Sydney Water’s WEPs. It was first deployed during the extreme raw water quality events of 2020, and the technology was progressively rolled out and fully implemented across all WFPs in 2024. Since its implementation, SWIFT has enabled Sydney Water to maintain stable plant performance through three major ‘one-in-100-year’ floods, ensuring uninterrupted supply of safe and clean drinking water to more than 5.6 million customers across Sydney. Through optimisation of existing WFPs, the SWIFT model has enabled a reduction in the required capacity of new pre-treatment upgrades, resulting in significant capital savings for future infrastructure. In parallel with digital optimisation initiatives, Sydney Water is making substantial capital investments to strengthen the resilience of its water treatment system against increasingly extreme and variable raw water quality. The centrepiece of this investment is a major new pre-treatment facility at Prospect WFP - Sydney’s largest WFP. The pre- treatment plant, using a high rate clarification process, will reduce raw water turbidity, colour, algae and organic matter, thereby improving the performance of the downstream WFP. This upgrade will significantly enhance raw water clarification by removing higher loads of sediment and turbidity, colour, as well as DOC and NOM before filtration. The new facility is designed specifically to manage the severe water quality deterioration associated with drought, bushfire aftermath and intense rainfall events. By stabilising and improving water quality, the upgrade will safeguard filtration performance and assure continuity of safe drinking water production during extreme events. The new pre- treatment facility is expected to be commissioned in 2027-28. Planned pre-treatment upgrades are also under construction across other key WFPs in Sydney Water, which will further strengthen Sydney’s water treatment resilience. All current pre-treatment upgrades are expected to be completed by 2030.
