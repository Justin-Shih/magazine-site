﻿## 新聞彙整 ### G7 承認土地退化與乾旱是全球安全風險 七國集團（G7）環境部長於 4 月 23 至 24 日在巴黎舉行會議，正式承認荒漠化、土地退化與乾旱是系統性的全球挑戰，也是放大安全風險的因素，並承諾加強土地復育、乾旱韌性與永續土地管理行動。 這些彼此連動的危機已經影響生態系、生計、糧食安全與水安全，並對經濟穩定與和平帶來越來越大的影響；在脆弱及受衝突影響的地區尤其如此。 部長們在共同宣言中強調，處理土地退化與安全之間的關聯具有戰略重要性。他們指出，環境壓力正在加劇資源競爭，並造成流離失所與不穩定風險。 過去六十年來，超過 40% 的國內衝突都與自然資源爭端有關，特別是土地與水，這凸顯土地退化與乾旱日益明顯的安全意涵。 土地退化已影響全球相當大比例的土地，最高可達 40%，每年造成估計 9,000 億美元損失，並在糧食系統、水資源可用性、經濟與生計之間引發連鎖衝擊。 聯合國防治荒漠化公約（UNCCD）執行秘書 Yasmine Fouad 歡迎這項宣言，但也表示，政治上的承認現在必須轉化為行動。她指出，土地退化與乾旱已不再是邊緣議題，而是正在塑造數百萬人的日常生活，影響他們能種什麼、能吃什麼，以及是否能留在自己的土地上。土地復育因此是對和平、韌性與長期穩定的投資；現在需要的是讓政治關注與所需的資金及夥伴關係相匹配，才能真正交付成果。 挑戰的規模使這份急迫性更加明顯。估計已有 32 億人生活在受土地退化影響的地區，這對糧食系統、生計和社會穩定形成日益沉重的壓力。部長們指出，土地復育與乾旱韌性的投資仍然不足且破碎，因此呼籲公共與私人資金更好地對齊，並加強國際金融機構之間的協調。 為支持這些承諾，法國主席國宣布多項倡議，包括 Nature & People Finance Alliance，目標是擴大對自然與生態系的公共及私人投資。 ### Lake Erie 吸引國際水質研究 根據非營利組織 Cleveland Water Alliance 的說法，Lake Erie 將成為世界上規模最大的數位連結淡水水體。該組織正與約 300 家公司、研究機構和政府單位合作，在湖泊及其周邊開發清潔水解方，把這座湖轉變為水研究設施。 這座北美五大湖之一的面積幾乎相當於比利時，也曾有污染歷史。為了處理這些問題，研究人員將部署數百個感測浮標，觀測並偵測大腸桿菌、藻華、濁度以及其他與水相關的因子，並與全球公司和研究者合作。 ### SePRO 收購 Earth Science Laboratories 美國公司 SePRO 宣布收購先進水處理技術專家 Earth Science Laboratories（ESL）。SePRO 專注於水域與土地復育，認為這項收購將強化其在飲用水、輸送系統與入侵物種管理方面的水處理產品組合。公司表示，擴大以科學為基礎的處理解方，目的在於更有力支持負責維持水系統清潔與安全的水管理者、市政單位與社區。 ### 聯合國秘書長呼籲回應非洲「氣候不正義」 聯合國秘書長 António Guterres 在衣索比亞 Addis Ababa 舉行的第 39 在氣候方面，他指出科學證據非常明確，地球即將超過巴黎協定中避免最嚴重氣候衝擊所需守住的 1.5°C 門檻。非洲正面臨高於全球平均的升溫速度，以及乾旱、洪水和致命高溫。他因此強調「氣候不正義」，並呼籲已開發國家將調適融資提高三倍，於 2035 年前每年為發展中國家動員 1.3 兆美元，並擴大 Loss and Damage Fund。 ### 330 萬肯亞人面臨急性飢餓風險 由 Action News G7 recognises land degradation and drought as global security risks Ministers at the Group of Seven (G7) Environment Ministerial meeting held in Paris on 23-24 April, formally recognised desertification, land degradation and drought as systemic global challenges and security risk multipliers, committing to strengthen action on land restoration, drought resilience and sustainable land management. These interlinked crises are already affecting ecosystems, livelihoods and food and water security, with growing implications for economic stability and peace, particularly in fragile and conflict affected regions. In their joint declaration, ministers stressed the strategic importance of addressing the land degradation security nexus, highlighting how environmental pressures are intensifying competition over resources, contributing to displacement and risks of instability. In the past six decades, over 40% of intrastate conflicts have been linked to disputes over natural resources - particularly land and water - underscoring the growing security implications of land degradation and drought. Land degradation already affects a significant share of the world’s land - up to 40% and costs an estimated $900 billion annually, with cascading impacts across food systems, water availability, economies and livelihoods. Welcoming the declaration, the Executive Secretary of the United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD), Yasmine Fouad, said this political recognition must now translate into action. She said: “Land degradation and drought are no longer marginal issues. They are already shaping the daily lives of millions of people, affecting what they can grow, what they can eat, and whether they can remain on their land. Restoring land is therefore an investment in peace, resilience and long-term stability. What is needed now is to match political attention with the financing and partnerships required to deliver results.” This urgency is underscored by the scale of the challenge. An estimated 3.2 billion people already live in areas affected by land degradation, placing increasing pressure on food systems, livelihoods and social stability. Ministers highlighted that investment in land restoration and drought resilience remains insufficient and fragmented, calling for stronger alignment of public and private finance and greater coordination across international financial institutions. In support of these commitments, the French Presidency announced initiatives, including the Nature & People Finance Alliance, aimed at scaling up public and private investment in nature and ecosystems. The World Bank Group, in partnership. with multilateral development banks, development finance institutions and key partners, has launched Water Forward, a global platform to help improve water security for one billion people by 2030. The platform will align policy reforms, financing, and partnerships to expand reliable water services and strengthen systems against droughts and floods. Water underpins health, food systems, energy, and an estimated 1.7 billion jobs worldwide - yet four billion people experience water scarcity. In many countries, unclear policies, weak regulations, and financially unsustainable utilities have slowed progress and deterred investment in the sector. Water Forward aims to address these challenges by helping developing countries build stronger, more reliable water systems that can unlock productivity, support livelihoods, and enable private investment. The initiative will support reforms to strengthen institutions, improve financial performance, and develop investment ready projects. Ajay Banga, President of the World Bank Group, said: “Water is foundational to how economies function. When water systems work, farmers produce, businesses operate, and cities attract investment. Our task now is to align reform, financing, and partnerships to deliver reliable water services at scale.” At the core of the initiative are country-led water compacts through which governments define reform priorities, commit to strengthening institutions, and establish investment pathways for their water sectors. Multilateral development banks, governments, philanthropies, and private sector actors are aligning financing and expertise to speed investment and implementation of projects that will boost reliable access to water. The World Bank Group is committed to delivering water security to 400 million people by 2030. With its additional partner commitments, Water Forward expects to reach more than one billion people.
