獅子山 2035 WASH 策略
Sierra Leone's 2035 WASH strategy

治理會員 / Governing Member

中文完整摘要說明

獅子山雖水資源相對豐富，卻因投資不足、制度薄弱、氣候變遷、都市化和人口成長而面臨經濟性缺水與 WASH 服務落差。2035 策略提出十年改革路徑，重點包括部門整合、融資協調、流域保護、城鄉供水、包容性衛生服務與固體廢棄物管理。

中文全文翻譯

﻿## 獅子山 2035 WASH 策略 Mohamed Bah 說明獅子山如何建立一條十年路徑，以實現安全且可靠的水、衛生與個人衛生服務。 獅子山擁有豐富資源，被認為是西非水壓力最低的國家之一。然而，這項天然優勢並沒有轉化為廣泛取得安全、可靠的水、衛生與個人衛生（WASH）服務。該國正面臨日益嚴重的經濟性缺水，原因包括投資不足、制度框架薄弱，以及氣候變遷、都市化和人口成長的共同影響。這些問題阻礙國家發展目標，也削弱人力資本發展。 全國基本用水可及率為 63%，但只有 23% 的人口能取得安全管理的用水。衛生服務取得也有限，25% 的人口仍露天排便，68% 缺乏基本衛生服務。此外，基本個人衛生服務的可及率極低，只有 12%。這些缺口造成重大的健康與經濟成本，降低教育成就，損害生態系，並降低生產力。 獅子山政府將水資源與衛生部門整併為單一部會，以強化部門凝聚力、制定政策方向並監督 WASH 部門；部長也與世界銀行、UNICEF 和 IWA 等策略夥伴合作。獅子山的 National Water Development Strategy 由總統 Julius Maada Bio 於 2025 年啟動，提供一套十年框架，以重新調整部門治理、融資和服務交付。其明確願景是：到 2035 年，透過高效且具韌性的水務制度，實現「水安全且衛生安全的獅子山」。該策略辨識出獅子山 WASH 部門面臨的主要挑戰。 ### 水資源管理薄弱 集水區保護薄弱、原水使用監管不足，以及監測有限，正在威脅水服務的永續性。快速都市化、森林砍伐與集水區侵占正在加速退化，而氣候變遷也增加季節變異和洪水風險。若不緊急投資於整合式水資源管理，現有系統的永續性與未來成長潛力都將面臨風險。 ### 基礎設施與營運不足 營運與維護實務不足，導致水基礎設施故障率偏高。這使供水者陷入惡性循環：服務品質差、收入低、投資有限，基礎設施進一步惡化。 ### 投資有限 依賴捐助者使該部門容易受到捐助優先事項變化影響，削弱政府執行長期策略投資的能力。缺乏專門預算項目，以及私人部門參與環境薄弱，也限制了融資選項。 ### 協調不足 部門機構之間破碎化，以及與發展夥伴合作有限，造成規劃與執行無序。這又因過度依賴短期專案而惡化；這些專案通常由立即需求推動，而非由策略目標引導。在缺乏統一長期願景的情況下，努力往往是反應式且破碎的。雖然這類專案可能提供暫時紓解，但並沒有處理阻礙 WASH 部門永續轉型的系統性問題。 ### 效率與能力缺口 關鍵水務公用事業與服務供應者的效率仍有改善空間。高非收益水率限制服務擴張：Guma Valley Water Company（GVWC）為 60%，Sierra Leone Water Company（SALWACO）為 30%；收費效率偏低也構成限制。核心問題是制度能力不足，以及對強化系統所需的人力與資源發展投資有限。 面對部門迫切挑戰，National Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Sector Development Plan（NWASHSDP）提供一套十年框架，目標是重振治理、強化融資機制並改善服務交付。這項雄心勃勃的倡議希望創造一個未來，使人人都能取得可及且永續的水、衛生與個人衛生服務，體現其對全國社區繁榮與福祉的整體願景。 該計畫以到 2035 年實現「透過高效且具韌性的水務制度，建立水安全且衛生安全的獅子山」為目標，願景圍繞四個相互連結的目標：第一，到 2035 年強化水資源管理並實現全民用水可及；第二，消除露天排便，實現全民取得衛生及其管理服務，並改善個人衛生可及；第三，強化所有水部門機構的制度與能力建設；第四，改善水、衛生與個人衛生部門融資。 要實現這些目標，所需總投資估計約為 17 億美元。關鍵在於，必須透過改革改善治理並降低低效率，才能釋放這筆資金。NWASHSDP 強調五項關鍵行動，以重新調整制度績效、擴大服務交付並確保永續融資。 ### 第一項優先行動：強化制度能力並提升 WASH 服務交付效率 這項行動強調進行全面能力評估，以強化各層級機構。它支持水資源與衛生部（MWRS）透過有針對性的技術協助、人員配置，以及規劃和監管工具發展來履行職責。此外，它也凸顯釐清制度角色、改善跨機構協調，以及在績效管理、採購、財務監督、監測與評估等領域建立長期能力的重要性。 地方議會，尤其是 Makeni、Bo 和 Port Loko 等快速成長城市，將獲得支持，以提升分權服務交付和監督能力。這項優先工作也旨在降低水務公用事業損失、提高客戶參與，並在適當情況下朝反映成本的水價邁進。 ### 第二項優先行動：促進並激勵關鍵政府行動者與發展夥伴之間的協調 這項行動的目標是簡化部門融資並提升投資影響。為處理破碎化問題，該倡議將正式化跨機構協調，並重組 Sector Coordination Committee，使地方政府、發展夥伴和公民社會團體納入其中。標準化的 WASH 資訊系統也將建立，以支援整合規劃和投資決策。 該策略還將透過增加財政轉移、改善服務供應者收入生成，以及透過公私夥伴關係動員私人資本，促進資金來源多元化。 ### 第三項優先行動：保護水資源並擴大城鄉供水 這項倡議旨在保護關鍵集水區，包括 Western Area Peninsula 和 Upper Moa Basin，並發展具韌性的城市供水系統。獅子山需要一套具氣候意識的水資源監測系統，以強化預報與調適策略。相關投資將聚焦於提高供水可用性與可靠性，尤其是在快速擴張的都市地區。 ### 第四項優先行動：投資包容性衛生與個人衛生服務 這項行動採取具性別回應的方式，優先處理學校與醫療設施中的 WASH。擴大投資將優先支持家庭和機構衛生基礎設施，包括建設糞污處理設施。 該計畫鼓勵以市場為基礎的衛生方法，以及與私人部門建立夥伴關係，確保農村和服務不足都市地區能取得個人衛生產品。行為改變宣導將針對 Western Area、Tonkolili 和 Kambia 等霍亂與腹瀉熱點；衛生設施也將被設計來回應婦女和女孩的需求。 ### 第五項優先行動：強化固體廢棄物管理 最後一項優先行動處理日益嚴重的固體廢棄物問題，尤其是在都市與城郊地區。它將支持社區型廢棄物收集，改善廢棄物運輸物流，並在 Freetown、Bo 和 Kenema 等城市投資工程化掩埋場或轉運站。 計畫將特別關注易淹水且人口密集的區域。它也強調有必要建立清楚的政策與法規，以促進廢棄物分類、回收和公私夥伴關係。這些努力將由公眾意識宣導補充，目的在於鼓勵行為改變，並提高固體廢棄物管理的問責。 ### 十年轉型已經展開 獅子山的 WASH 策略為實現公平取得水與衛生服務提供了轉型性跨越。透過大量資金、制度改革，以及建立在國家自主和國際支持上的十年計畫，這項倡議為包容性發展、更好的健康成果，以及社會經濟賦權奠定了強有力方向，尤其有助於婦女和服務不足社區。 作者 Mohamed Bah 是 Rural Water Supply Network（RWSN）導師，也是獅子山水資源部監測與評估專案官員。

Original Full Text

Sierra Leone’s Mohamed Bah describes how Sierra Leone is creating a 10-year pathway to achieve secure and safe WASH services. ith abundant resources, Sierra Leone is considered one of the least water- stressed countries in West Africa. However, this natural advantage has not resulted in widespread access to safe and reliable water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) services. The country faces growing economic water scarcity caused by underinvestment, weak institutional frameworks and the combined impacts of climate change, urbanisation and population growth. These issues hinder the country’s development goals and weaken human capital development. Access to basic water nationwide is 63%, but only 23% of the population has access to safely , managed water. Sanitation access is limited, with 25% of people practising open defecation and 68% lacking basic sanitation services. Additionally, access to basic hygiene services is critically low at just 12%. These gaps lead to significant health and economic costs, reduce educational achievement, harm ecosystems and decrease productivity. The government of Sierra Leone merged the Ministry of Water Resources and Sanitation into a single ministry to reinforce sectoral cohesion, set policy directives and oversee the WASH sector, with its Minister collaborating with strategic partners such as the World Bank, UNICEF and IWA. Launched by President Julius Maada Bio in 2025, Sierra Leone’s National Water Development Strategy provides a 10-year framework to realign sector governance, funding and service delivery, with a clear vision for “a water-secure and sanitation-safe Sierra Leone through efficient and resilient water institutions by 2035”. The strategy identifies the following major challenges faced by the WASH sector in Sierra Leone. Weak water resource management Weak watershed protection, poor regulation of raw water use and limited monitoring are threatening the sustainability of water services. Rapid urbanisation, deforestation and catchment encroachment are accelerating degradation, while climate change is increasing Freetown, Sierra Leone seasonal variability and flood risks. Without urgent investment in integrated water resource management, the sustainability of current systems and the potential for future growth are at risk. Infrastructure and operational inadequacies Inadequate operations and maintenance practices lead to a high failure rate of water infrastructure. This traps water providers into a vicious cycle of poor service, low revenue, limited investments and worsening infrastructure. Limited investments Reliance on donors leaves the sector vulnerable to changing donor priorities, weakening government's ability to execute strategic long- term investments. The lack of a dedicated budget line and a weak environment for private sector involvement limit finance options. Limited coordination Fragmentation among sector agencies and limited cooperation with development partners lead to iti § disorganised planning and execution. This is worsened by an over-dependence on short-term projects, which are often driven by immediate needs rather than strategic goals. Efforts tend to be reactive and fragmented without a unified long-term vision. While such projects may provide temporary relief, they do not tackle the systemic issues that hinder sustainable WASH sector transformation. Inefficiencies and capacity gaps Efficiencies could be improved by key water utilities and service providers. High non-revenue water (NRW) rates — 60% for the Guma Valley Water Company (GVWC) and 30% for the Sierra Leone Water Company (SALWACO) - along with low collection efficiency limit service expansion. The core issues are insufficient institutional capacity and limited investment in system-strengthening human and resource development. In response to the sector’s pressing challenges, the National Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Sector Development Plan (NWASHSDP) provides a 10-year framework aimed at revitalising governance, enhancing funding mechanisms and improving service delivery. This ambitious initiative aspires to create a future where accessible and sustainable water, sanitation and hygiene services are a reality for all, reflecting its overarching vision of prosperity and well-being for communities across the nation. Aiming to achieve “a water- secure and sanitation-safe Sierra Leone through efficient and resilient water institutions by 2035”, the plan’s vision is centred on four interconnected goals: 1, Enhancing water resource management and achieving Only 23% of the population has access to safely managed water universal access to water by 2035. 2. Eliminating open defecation and achieving universal access to sanitation and its management while improving access to hygiene by 2035. 3, Institutional strengthening and capacity building for all water sector institutions. 4. Improving water, sanitation, and hygiene sector financing. The total investment required to achieve this is estimated to be approximately $1.7 billion. Crucially, reforms to improve governance and reduce inefficiencies will be required to unlock this funding. The NWASHSDP highlights five key actions to realign institutional performance, expand service delivery and secure sustainable financing. The first priority is to strengthen institutional capacity and improve WASH service delivery efficiency. This action emphasises a comprehensive capacity assessment to strengthen institutions at all levels. It supports the Ministry of Water Resources and Sanitation (MWRS) in executing its mandates through targeted technical assistance, staffing and the development of planning and regulatory tools. Additionally, it highlights the importance of clarifying institutional roles, improving inter-agency coordination and building long- term capacity in areas such as performance-based management, procurement, financial oversight and monitoring and evaluation. Local councils — especially in rapidly growing cities such as Makeni, Bo and Port Loko — will receive support to enhance decentralised service delivery and oversight. This priority also aims to reduce water utility losses, increase customer engagement and move toward cost- reflective tariffs where appropriate. The second priority action is to facilitate and incentivise coordination among key Governing Member government actors and development partners to streamline sector financing and the impact of investment. To address fragmentation, this initiative will formalise inter-agency coordination and reorganise the Sector Coordination Committee to include local governments, development partners and civil society groups. A standardised WASH information system will be established to support integrated planning and investment decisions. The strategy will also promote diversified funding sources through increased fiscal transfers, improved revenue generation by service providers and the mobilisation of private capital via public-private partnerships. The third priority action is to safeguard water resources and expand urban and rural water supply. This initiative aims to safeguard key watersheds, including the Western Area Peninsula and Upper Moa Basin, and to develop resilient urban water systems. There is a need for a climate-aware water resource monitoring system to enhance forecasting and adaptation strategies. Investments in this effort will focus on increasing the availability and reliability of water supplies, especially in rapidly expanding urban areas. The fourth priority action is to invest in inclusive sanitation and hygiene services with a gender- responsive approach, giving priority to WASH in schools and healthcare facilities. Scaled investment will prioritise household and institutional sanitation infrastructure, including the construction of faecal sludge treatment facilities. The plan encourages market- based sanitation approaches and partnerships with the private sector to ensure hygiene products are accessible in rural and underserved urban areas. Behaviour change campaigns will target cholera and diarrhoea hotspots such as the Western Area, Tonkolili, and Freetown, Western Area, Sierra Leone Kambia, and sanitation facilities will be designed to address the needs of women and girls. The final priority action is to strengthen solid waste management. This initiative addresses rising solid waste issues, especially in urban and peri-urban areas. It will support community-based waste collection, improve waste transport logistics, and invest in engineered landfills or transfer stations in cities such as Freetown, Bo, and Kenema. Particular focus will be given to flood-prone and densely populated zones. The plan also stresses the need to establish clear policies and regulations to promote waste segregation, recycling, and public- private partnerships. These efforts will be complemented by public awareness campaigns designed to encourage behavioural change and increase accountability in solid waste management. Set for 10 years of transformation Sierra Leone’s WASH strategy provides a transformative leap toward, fulfilling equitable access to water and sanitation. With substantial funding, institutional reforms, and a decade-long plan anchored in national ownership and international support, the initiative sets a powerful course for inclusive development, better health outcomes, and socioeconomic empowerment, especially for women and underserved communities. ©
