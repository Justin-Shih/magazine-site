歐洲氣溫持續上升
European temperatures on the rise

分析 / Analysis

中文完整摘要說明

本文介紹 European State of the Climate 2025 報告，指出歐洲作為全球升溫最快的大陸，正面臨創紀錄熱浪、海表溫度上升、冰川退縮、河川流量偏低、野火擴大與生物多樣性壓力。報告強調氣候變遷已是當下現實，歐洲需要加速調適、自然復育與以科學為基礎的政策行動。

中文全文翻譯

﻿## 歐洲氣溫持續上升 Erika Yarrow-Soden 報導。 《European State of the Climate 2025》報告指出，從地中海到北極，歐洲都出現創紀錄熱浪；同時，歐洲各地冰川正在萎縮，積雪覆蓋也持續下降。報告也警告，海表溫度創下紀錄，野火延燒面積達到有紀錄以來最大範圍，河川則受到全年流量低於平均值的影響。 ### 氣候指標的完整概覽 《European State of the Climate》（ESOTC）2025 報告由歐洲中期天氣預報中心（ECMWF）與世界氣象組織（WMO）共同製作，匯集約 100 位科學貢獻者的工作，為全球升溫最快的大陸提供關鍵氣候指標變化的完整概覽。報告涵蓋寒冷環境、海洋生態系、河川與湖泊，以及野火風險等面向。 ### 高於平均的氣溫 報告發現，2025 年至少 95% 的歐洲地區經歷高於平均值的年均氣溫。亞北極的 Fennoscandia 出現創紀錄的三週熱浪，北極圈附近及圈內地區的氣溫超過 30°C。歐洲所有區域的冰川都出現淨質量損失，其中冰島記錄到有史以來第二大的冰川流失。 歐洲區域的年度海表溫度也是有紀錄以來最高，86% 的區域至少經歷「強」等級的海洋熱浪。歐洲全年有 11 個月的河川流量低於平均值，70% 的河川記錄到低於平均的年度流量。然而，風暴和洪水仍影響了歐洲數千人，只是極端降雨與洪水的分布範圍不如近年廣泛。 ECMWF 氣候策略負責人 Samantha Burgess 表示，ESOTC 2025 描繪出嚴峻圖像，氣候變遷的速度要求更迫切的行動。隨著氣溫上升、野火和乾旱普遍發生，證據已非常明確：氣候變遷不是未來威脅，而是現在的現實。面對生物多樣性喪失的衝擊，調適速度需要跟上清潔能源轉型的速度，同時確保健全科學持續支撐政策與決策。 ### 創紀錄的海洋高溫與海洋暖化 全球海洋吸收了人為溫室氣體排放造成的約 90% 多餘熱量。2025 年，歐洲海洋區域經歷有紀錄以來最高的年度海表溫度，這也是連續第四年創下暖化紀錄。這項長期趨勢對生物多樣性、物種與棲地造成負面影響。海洋熱浪也可能與陸地熱浪同時發生，使溫度與濕度更加極端。 ### 氣候條件對歐洲水資源造成壓力 報告發現，歐洲約 70% 的河川流量低於平均值，而 2025 年也是自 1992 年以來土壤濕度最低的三個年份之一。2025 年 5 月，約一半歐洲地區，也就是 53%，受到乾旱條件影響。這些模式反映多種因素的組合，包括大氣環流、降雨變異，以及更長期的氣候趨勢。 ### 氣溫升高與野火加劇 2025 年，歐洲廣泛出現溫暖條件，至少 95% 的大陸地區記錄到高於平均值的氣溫。熱浪影響歐洲大片地區，從地中海一路延伸到北極，包括歐洲有紀錄以來第二嚴重的熱浪，以及亞北極 Fennoscandia 在 7 月經歷的最長、最嚴重熱浪。歐洲多數地區也出現比平均更多的「強」熱壓力日數，其中西班牙南部與東部比平均多出最多 50 天。 炎熱且乾燥的條件造成歐洲創紀錄的野火活動。總計約 103 萬公頃土地在歐洲被燒毀，面積大於賽普勒斯，野火排放也達到有紀錄以來最高。西班牙受到的衝擊尤其嚴重，約占排放量的一半。除西班牙外，賽普勒斯、英國、荷蘭和德國也記錄到各自有史以來最高的野火排放。 野火也對生物多樣性構成重大風險，而泥炭地野火可能釋放大量儲存碳，進一步放大氣候變遷。歐盟執委會已將上升中的野火風險列為關鍵優先事項。政府間氣候變遷專門委員會（IPCC）第六次評估報告的預測顯示，歐洲所有區域的野火威脅都可能增加。 ### 影響生物多樣性的氣候衝擊 2025 年的許多極端事件，包括乾旱、野火、陸地與海洋熱浪，都衝擊了歐洲海洋和陸域生態系的生物多樣性。此外，由於棲地縮小與移動、暖化造成季節模式中斷，以及降水變化，自然正承受更大壓力。 報告列出氣候變遷影響敏感生態系的具體案例，例如地中海海草床受到海洋熱浪影響，以及泥炭地野火。歐洲環境治理已越來越承認氣候與生物多樣性之間的密切關聯，並開始設計協調性的「自然－氣候」方法，以強化生態系韌性。 此外，歐盟已承諾具法律拘束力的目標，要大規模復育生態系，包括到 2030 年復育至少 20% 的陸地與海域，並到 2050 年復育所有「有需要」的生態系。這項工作由 European Green Deal 與 EU Climate Law 支撐，將生物多樣性保護嵌入更廣泛的氣候策略中。海草床復育等自然本位解方，也因其支持生物多樣性、儲存碳並強化海岸韌性的角色而受到重視。 雖然這些步驟代表重要行動，但報告強調，歐洲的進展必須加速。氣候變遷已經產生影響，並正在劇烈改變棲地、生活與生計。

Analysis Analysis European temperatures on the rise By Erika Yarrow-Soden The European State of the Climate 2025 report highlights record heatwaves from. the Mediterranean to the Arctic, with glaciers shrinking and snow cover declining across Europe. The report also warns of record sea surface temperatures, wildfires extending across the largest area on record, and rivers impacted by below- average annual flows. Comprehensive overview of climate indicators The European State of the Climate (ESOTC) 2025 report, produced by the European: Centre for Medium-Range Weather Forecasts (ECMWF) and the World Meteorological Organization (WMO), brings together the work of approximately 100 scientific contributors and provides a comprehensive overview of key changes in climate indicators for the world’s fastest warming continent, including cold environments, marine ecosystems, rivers. and lakes, and wildfire risk. Above average temperatures The report finds that at least 95% of Europe experienced above-average annual temperatures in 2025. A record three-week heatwave affected sub-Arctic Fennoscandia, with temperatures near to and within the Arctic Circle exceeding 30°C. Glaciers in all European regions saw a net mass loss, with Iceland recording its second largest glacier loss on record. The annual sea surface temperature for the European region was the highest on record, with 86% of the region experiencing at least ‘strong’ marine heatwaves. River flows were below average for 11 months of the year across Europe, with 70% of rivers recording below-average annual flows. However, storms and flooding affected thousands of people across Europe, though extreme rainfall and flooding were less widespread than in recent years. Samantha Burgess, strategic lead for climate at ECMWF, says: “The ESOTC 2025 paints a stark picture - the pace of climate change demands more urgent action. With rising temperatures and widespread wildfires and drought, the evidence is unequivocal; climate change is not a future threat, it is our present reality. In confronting the impact on biodiversity loss, we need to match the speed of adaptation happening in the clean energy transition while ensuring robust science continues to underpin our policies and decisions.” Record marine heat and ocean warming The global ocean has absorbed about 90% of the excess heat caused by human- induced greenhouse gas emissions. In 2025, the European ocean region experienced its: highest annual sea surface temperature on record, marking the fourth consecutive year of record warmth. This long-term trend negatively affects biodiversity, species and habitats. Marine heatwaves can also coincide with heatwaves over land - contributing to more extreme temperatures and humidity. Climate conditions place pressure on Europe’s water The report finds that across Europe around 70% of rivers experienced below average flows, while 2025 was one of the three driest years for soil moisture since 1992. In May 2025, around half of Europe (53%). was affected by drought conditions. These patterns reflect a combination of factors including atmospheric circulation and rainfall variability, and longer term climate trends. Increasing temperatures and intensifying wildfires Across Europe, 2025 saw widespread warm conditions with above-average temperatures recorded across at least 95%. of the continent. Heatwaves affected large parts of Europe, from the Mediterranean to 66 Wildfire threats are likely to increase across all regions of Europe the Arctic, including Europe’s second most severe heatwave on record and sub-Arctic Fennoscandia’s longest and most severe heatwave, in July. Much of Europe also experienced more days than average with at least ‘strong’ heat stress, with southern and eastern Spain recording up to 50 more days than average. Hot and dry conditions contributed to record wildfire activity across Europe. In total, a record area of around 1.03 million hectares burned across Europe - an area larger than Cyprus - with wildfire emissions reaching their highest levels on record. The impact on Spain was particularly severe and accounted for around half of the emissions. Alongside Spain, Cyprus, the UK, the Netherlands and Germany recorded their highest wildfire emissions on record. Wildfires also pose a significant risk to biodiversity, and peatland wildfires can release large stores of carbon, further amplifying climate change. The European Commission has identified rising wildfire risk as a key priority, as projections by. the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change’s (IPPC) Sixth Assessment Report show that wildfire threats are likely to increase across all regions of Europe. Climate impacts affecting biodiversity Numerous extreme events in 2025 - including drought, wildfires, and land and marine heatwaves - impacted biodiversity in Europe's marine and terrestrial ecosystems. Additionally, nature has become under increased stress because of shrinking and shifting habitats, disruption of seasonal patterns caused by warmer temperatures: and changes in precipitation. The report: outlines concrete examples of climate change impacts on sensitive ecosystems, such as marine heatwaves on seagrass meadows in the Mediterranean Sea, and peatland wildfires. Environmental governance in Europe has increasingly recognised the close links between climate and biodiversity, with coordinated ‘nature- climate’ approaches being designed to strengthen ecosystem resilience, In addition, the European Union (EU) has committed to legally binding targets to restore ecosystems at scale, including at least 20% of land and sea areas by 2030 and all ecosystems ‘in need’ by 2050. This work is underpinned by the European Green Deal and EU Climate Law, which embed biodiversity protection within wider climate strategies. Additionally, nature- based solutions such as restoring seagrass meadows are being recognised for their role in supporting biodiversity, storing carbon, and strengthening coastal resilience. While these steps represent important action, this report highlights that progress in Europe must accelerate.
