中文全文翻譯

# 慶祝 Integrated Environmental Technology 25 週年 IWA「Integrated Environmental Technology」（IET）出版系列的系列主編 Piet Lens 回顧這套書在過去 25 年中的發展。 IWA 的 Integrated Environmental Technology 書系，從多學科且整合性的角度，處理環境技術領域中的關鍵主題與議題。這套書最重要的特色，是每一冊都跨越科學學科之間的界線，展現跨學科特質，同時也整合科學洞見與實務知識，形成跨越研究與應用場域的知識連結。這套書系於 2000 年創立，當時的核心信念是：面對全球正在承受的重大污染問題與挑戰，整合式方法是最可行的解方。 ## 人類世留下的課題 自 IET 書系創立以來，地球已經歷重大變化。人口成長、資源需求增加與生物多樣性下降，使環境問題持續放大。早在 1962 年 Rachel Carson 的《寂靜的春天》，以及 1972 年羅馬俱樂部的《成長的極限》報告，就已提出警訊。然而到了 2026 年，我們仍看到一個努力在經濟成長、環境劣化與人類福祉之間取得平衡的世界。 目前沒有任何國家能在滿足人民需求的同時，不越過地球的生物物理邊界。更重要的是，與 1972 年相比，今日造成不可持續資源使用的主要驅動因素，已不再只是發展不足與匱乏，而是過度發展與富裕。如今許多人的生活比父母當年所能想像的更好。我們壽命更長，也能取得更多東西：更多乾淨的水、物質商品、食物與醫療服務。 然而，並不是所有人都如此。數十億人仍然缺乏乾淨水源與衛生設施。同時，我們也正面臨 1972 年時尚未充分認識的地球系統臨界點。到今天，我們已經理解，有些臨界點可能會在我們有生之年被不可逆地觸發，包括永久凍土流失、冰蓋瓦解，以及雨林退化。 ## 打開更好未來的鑰匙 IET 書系不只是技術手冊。它綜合科學研究、帶入新觀念，並因此形塑我們如何思考資源在永續未來中的使用與再使用。每一冊都匯集世界各地專家，針對特定主題呈現基礎原理與應用技術的完整組合。書中回顧現行實務與最新技術狀態，介紹分析、科學與生物科技的新發展；更關鍵的是，每一冊都把主題放在相鄰的科學、社會與經濟領域中討論，從真正整合的視角提出解決方案。 IET 書系顯示，人類比以往更有能力追求地球上的永續未來。它推動水、空氣、固體廢棄物，以及土壤與沉積物整治上的典範轉移：從污染控制走向污染預防與資源回收；從提高回收效率、開創資源新用途，進一步納入循環性，也就是設計能讓資源持續流通的系統；最後走向「足夠」與「可以」的概念，重新思考在資源受限的世界中，什麼才是足夠。 ## 製程基礎 從廢棄物中回收資源，既是科學探索，也是商業機會。可使用的回收技術相當多元，包括高溫製程、化學製程、電化學製程與生物製程。高溫製程利用高熱來濃縮並轉化礦石與殘餘物；化學製程則依靠水相化學，例如浸出、溶劑萃取、離子交換與吸附；電化學製程使用電力進行萃取或精煉；生物製程則運用微生物與植物的能力。 IET 書系幾乎每一本都會提供與該主題相關的重要製程基礎導論。每一種路徑都有取捨，因此 IET 書籍也經常指出，許多情況下未來可能在於混合系統，例如把高溫製程與微生物結合，或把化學與生物學結合，以最大化效率並最小化環境衝擊。 其中尤其值得注意的是生物製程的潛力。數百年來，人類一直利用微生物與植物，以及它們的代謝作用，來達成人類目的。不過，要讓生物系統達到商業規模，仍是常見的科學挑戰。因為生物與生命會持續變化，要預測活體生物與微生物製程如何從實驗台放大到反應器，再到實地場域，本身就是一項挑戰。即便如此，土木與環境工程師已學會在每天僅掌握基本微生物功能資訊的情況下，處理龐大體積的廢水。 除了生物製程的技術潛力之外，IET 書系也展示了生物反應器中令人著迷的微生物生理學與微生物生態世界。書中詳細討論特殊的微生物代謝，例如厭氧氨氧化、聚磷菌、厭氧甲烷氧化、電纜細菌與微生物巨型化；同時也討論植物特徵，包括水通道蛋白與金屬結合蛋白。 ## 綠色水處理流程 除了確保所有人都能取得並永續使用水與衛生服務外，水務部門也應走向更綠色的路徑，降低溫室氣體排放，協助減緩氣候變遷的部分衝擊。與此同時，人口結構變化、都市化與工業化的結合，加劇了水資源可用性與人為水污染的壓力。此外，循環經濟與零污染的推動，也要求水務部門提出新的綠色方法，以降低能源、化學品與原物料消耗，同時提高回收與再利用。 「綠色水處理流程」指的是飲用水供應與廢水管理中的流程，這些流程包含自然為本解方，且簡單但穩健，消耗較少化學品與能源，或促進能源與資源的回收、循環與再利用。為了強化後續發展的可能性，IET 書系推出了相關主題的多冊出版品。 ## 新興污染物處理的新方法 供水與廢水後處理正面臨新興污染物的重大威脅，例如有機微污染物、微塑膠與奈米塑膠，以及病毒。先進處理技術使處理鏈變得更長、更複雜，也大幅增加碳足跡。這一冊 IET 書籍討論如何導入有利於簡單、穩健且具韌性的飲用水系統的流程，同時降低碳足跡。 ## 生活污水處理與再利用 相較於廣泛使用的集中式廢水處理系統，綠色廢水管理可能提供新的技術與系統方向。從技術觀點來看，處理技術的選擇會顯著影響放流水品質、能源與化學品消耗，以及能源與資源回收的潛力。從系統觀點來看，我們也必須思考：集中式系統是否真的提供了綠色解方？或者分散式系統是否會是廢水管理更好的選擇？ ## 水務部門中的能源與營養鹽回收 能源與營養鹽回收已討論相當長一段時間，也正逐漸進入實務應用。然而到目前為止，其應用仍然有限。這一冊 IET 書籍概述目前用於回收氮與磷的技術，同時也討論甲烷回收。 ## 循環性與再生式設計 在當代社會中，廢棄物與污染是被普遍接受的概念，也通常被視為生活中不可避免的事實，是需要管理的問題。近年來，線性的生產與消費系統受到更多質疑。這些系統從資源開採開始，以廢棄物處置結束，並因此打斷自然循環。 以能源系統為例，全球大部分能源仍透過燃燒化石燃料產生，並把二氧化碳與其他溫室氣體作為廢棄物排放到大氣中。全球大部分食物也透過農業實務生產，這些實務從環境中抽取營養鹽與水來進行生產，同時把污染物釋放回環境。這些線性過程打亂地球的能源、水、營養鹽與岩石循環，進而造成能源、水、食物與財務安全上的不確定。 循環性被提出作為線性系統的解方，也為人類帶來希望。以幾乎不產生廢棄物的方式使用與再使用自然資本，使生產與消費能維持在地球自然循環之內。通往循環經濟的道路，要求我們在生產流程與消費系統中，從設計階段就把廢棄物排除。 即使到了 2026 年，我們在邁向循環經濟的道路上仍然掙扎；困難並不只在工程挑戰，更多是在社會經濟與制度挑戰。循環經濟遠遠不只是回收。它涵蓋許多其他策略，用來建立循環並延長材料與產品的可使用壽命。IET 書系提供許多案例，顯示廢棄物與廢水如何成為再生能源、商品與高科技材料的來源，證明廢物流可以轉化為下一世代材料的原料，不只在回收層面閉合循環，也在創新層面閉合循環。 過去二十五年，永續性受到越來越多關注，「永續設計」一詞也逐漸擴展，納入再生式設計。然而，永續設計的問題在於，它往往忽略人類已在自然環境中造成的危機。為了補償既有損害，我們必須超越「中性」或永續的方法，改以對周遭環境產生正向影響為目標。 未來要求我們超越永續性，思考整個生態系，以及我們所建造的基礎設施如何整合進生態圈。這不只從住宅與建築開始，也包括廢水與固體廢棄物處理設施。不同於以永續為目標設計的基礎設施，再生式環境技術的設計與營運，是為了逆轉生態損害，並對自然環境產生淨正面影響。 再生式設計要求建築與基礎設施成為場所、基地、動植物與生態系的延伸。因此，建築與廢棄物處理設施可以成為更大系統的一部分，除了提供傳統建築與基礎設施功能外，也協助生產並共享乾淨水源、能源與食物等資源。 更多關於 IWA Integrated Environmental Technology 書系的資訊，可參考 IWA Publishing 的相關專頁：iwaponline.com/collection/45469/Integrated-Environmental-Technology-Book-Series。

Circular economy Circular economy Celebrating 25 years of Integrated Environmental Technology Piet Lens, series editor, looks back on how |WA’s Integrated Environmental Technology series of publications has developed over the past 25 years. WA’s Integrated Environmental Technology (IET) book series addresses key themes and issues in the field of environmental technology from a multidisciplinary and integrated perspective. The key feature of this book series is that each volume transcends the boundaries between scientific disciplines (interdisciplinarity) and ensures integration of scientific insights and practical knowledge (transdisciplinarity). The series was established in 2000 with the vision that an integrated approach is the most viable solution to the major pollution issues and challenges that the globe currently faces. The legacy of the Anthropocene Since the foundation of the IET book series the planet has seen major changes, with environmental problems magnified by population growth, increased resource requirements and a decline in biodiversity. Despite wake-up calls, such as the book Silent Spring by Rachel Carson in 1962 and the report Limits to Growth by the Club of Rome in 1972, we see in 2026 a world struggling to balance economic growth, environmental degradation and human well-being. Currently, no country is delivering what its citizens need without transgressing the biophysical planetary boundaries. Importantly, compared with 1972, over- development and affluence rather than underdevelopment and scarcity are now the drivers for unsustainable resource use. Nowadays, many people lead better lives than their parents expected for them. We live longer, with access to more - more clean water, material goods, food and healthcare. However, many people do not. For instance, billions of People still live without access to clean water and sanitation. And we are confronted with tipping points in the Earth’s systems that we were unaware of in 1972. To date, we realise some tipping points might be irreversibly triggered during our lifetime, including permafrost loss, ice sheet disintegration and rainforest dieback. Keys to unlocking a better future More than just technical manuals, the IET book series synthesises scientific research and brings new ideas, thus shaping how we think about the use and reuse of resources ina sustainable future. Each volume brings together world experts, presenting a comprehensive blend of fundamental principles and applied technologies for each topic. Current practices and the state-of: the-art are reviewed, new developments in analytics, science and biotechnology are presented and, crucially, the theme of each volume is presented in relation to adjacent scientific, social and economic fields to provide solutions from a truly integrated perspective. The IET book series shows we are better equipped than ever to pursue a sustainable future on Earth. It advances paradigm shifts in remediating water, air and solid waste, as well as soils and sediments — moving away from pollution control towards pollution prevention and resource recovery (improving recovery and innovating new uses), blending in circularity (designing systems that keep resources in circulation), towards sufficiency and ‘okayness’ (rethinking the concept of ‘enough’ in a resource-constrained world). Process fundamentals Recovering resources from waste is both a scientific quest and a business opportunity. The arsenal of recovery techniques is diverse and includes pyro (processes that harness high heat to concentrate and convert ores and residues), chemo (processes relying on aqueous chemistry such as leaching, solvent extraction, ion exchange and adsorption), electro (processes that employ electricity to extract or refine), and bio (processes tapping into the ingenuity of microbes and plants). Almost every book in the IET series provides introductory chapters on the most important process fundamentals of relevance. Each pathway has trade-offs, and the IET books often recognise that in many cases the future may lie in hybrid systems, for example pairing pyro with microbes or chemistry with biology, to maximise efficiency and minimise environmental impact. Of particular note is the potential of bioprocesses. For centuries, humans have exploited microbes and plants - and their metabolism — for human gain. Achieving commercial scale with biological systems is a common scientific hurdle to overcome. Because biology and life change constantly, predicting how living organisms and microbial processes will scale from the bench to a reactor to the field, is a challenge. Nevertheless, civil and environmental engineers have learned how to treat enormous volumes of wastewater each day with only basic information about microbial functions, In addition to the technical potential of bioprocesses, the TET book series also illustrates the fascinating world of microbial physiology and microbial ecology present in bioreactors, with special microbial metabolisms (such as anammox, phosphorous accumulating bacteria, anaerobic methane oxidation, cable bacteria and microbial giantism) and plant features (including aquaporins and metal binding proteins) discussed in detail. Besides ensuring availability and sustainability of water and sanitation for all, the water sector should move towards a greener route with reduced greenhouse gas emissions, helping to alleviate some of the impacts of climate change. Meanwhile, the combination of demographic developments, urbanisation and industrialisation have intensified pressure on water availability and anthropogenic water contamination. In addition, the drive for a circular economy approach and zero pollution require the water sector to come up with new and green approaches to reduce energy, chemical and raw material consumption, while increasing their recovery and reuse. ‘Green water processes’ refer to the processes in drinking water supply and wastewater management that include nature-based solutions, and that are simple but robust, involve less consumption of chemicals and energy, and/or promote recovery, recycling and reuse of energy and resources. To enhance the possibilities for further development, the IET series featured the following volumes: Novel approaches for treatment of emerging contaminants Water supply and wastewater post- treatment face great threats from new contaminants, such as organic micro-pollutants, micro/nano- plastics and viruses. Advanced treatments are making the treatment chain extra-long, complex, and substantially increase the carbon footprint. This IET volume addresses how processes can be implemented, favouring simple, robust, resilient drinking water systems, while reducing carbon footprints. Domestic wastewater treatment and reuse Compared with the widely used centralised wastewater treatment systems, green wastewater management may offer new technological and systematic directions. From a technological perspective, the choice of treatment technologies has significant influence on effluent quality, energy and chemical consumption, and the potential for energy and resource recovery. And from a systematic perspective, we also have to consider whether centralised systems offer a green solution, or if decentralised systems offer a better choice for wastewater management? Energy and nutrient recovery in the water sector Energy and nutrient recovery have been discussed for quite a long time and are gradually coming into practice. However, so far, their application is still limited. This IET volume provides an overview of current technologies used for the recovery of nitrogen and phosphorus, along with methane recovery. In contemporary society, waste and pollution are accepted concepts and are generally regarded as essential facts of life - problems that need to be managed. In recent years, questions have been raised about linear systems of production and consumption, which begin with resource extraction and end with waste disposal - disrupting nature’s cycles. In the energy system, for instance, most of the world’s energy is produced by burning fossil fuels, disposing carbon dioxide and other greenhouse gases as waste into the atmosphere. And most of the world’s food is produced by agricultural practices that extract nutrients and water for production, while releasing pollutants into the environment. These linear processes disrupt the planet’s energy, water, nutrient and rock cycles, thus creating energy, water, food and financial insecurities. Circularity was proposed as the solution for linear systems, and the concept brought hope to humanity. Using and reusing natural capital with little to no waste enables production and consumption to be sustained within the planet’s natural cycles. The path to a circular economy requires waste to be designed out of production processes and consumption systems. Still in 2026, we struggle on the path towards a circular economy, not so much with regards to the engineering challenge it brings, but more with the socio-economic and institutional challenges. The circular economy is far more than just recycling. It encompasses many other strategies to create loops and extend the usable lifetime of materials and products. The IET series provides many examples that show how waste and wastewater can be a source of renewable energy, commodities and high-tech materials, testifying that waste streams can become feedstocks for next-generation materials, closing the loop not just in recycling, but also in innovation. In the past two and a half decades, sustainability has received increasing attention, and the term ‘sustainable design’ has become broadened to include regenerative design. However, the problem with sustainable design is that it ignores the crisis that mankind has created in the natural environment. To compensate for this existing damage, we need to push beyond a ‘neutral’ or sustainable approach and instead aim for a target that achieves a positive impact on our surroundings. The future demands that we go beyond sustainability and consider the whole ecosystem, and how the infrastructure we build can be integrated into the ecosphere, beginning with our houses and buildings, but also wastewater and solid waste treatment facilities. In contrast to sustainably designed infrastructure, regenerative environmental technologies are designed and operated to reverse ecological damage and have a net-positive impact on the natural environment. Regenerative design demands architecture and infrastructure to be an extension of the place, the site, the flora and fauna, and the ecosystem. Buildings and waste treatment facilities can thus become a part of a larger system, helping to produce and share resources such as clean water, energy and food, next to the traditional functions architecture and the infrastructure provides.
