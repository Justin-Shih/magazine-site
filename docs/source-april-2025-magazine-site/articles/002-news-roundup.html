中文全文翻譯

# 新聞彙整 ## 全球研究發現氣候變遷正在擾亂地球水循環 一份新報告發現，全球暖化正改變地球水循環──加劇風暴、洪水與乾旱，影響整個生態系及數十億人口。 這份《2024 全球水文監測報告》（2024 Global Water Monitor Report）由來自澳洲、沙烏地阿拉伯、中國、德國及其他地區大學的國際研究團隊編製，運用數千個地面測站與衛星資料，評估降雨、土壤濕度、河川流量與洪水等關鍵水文變數。 研究人員警告，全球暖化正在改變水在地球上的移動方式。2024 年是有紀錄以來最熱的一年，當年的水災使 4,000 萬人流離失所，並造成超過 5,500 億美元的經濟損失。 持續使用化石燃料造成的升溫，會以多種方式擾亂水循環：較暖的空氣能容納更多水氣，導致更猛烈的降雨；較暖的海洋會為颶風與颱風提供更多能量；全球暖化除了擾亂降雨型態外，也因土壤水分蒸發增加而加劇乾旱。 報告作者警告，將 2025 年季節氣候預報與目前環境狀況一併評估後顯示，南美洲北部、非洲南部與亞洲部分地區的乾旱可能惡化；薩赫勒地區（非洲的一個生物地理區）與歐洲等較濕潤地區，則可能面臨升高的洪水風險。 這是第三份年度報告，延續前兩年的工作，概述 2024 年全球水循環的狀態、辨識關鍵趨勢並分析重大水文事件。 澳洲國立大學水科學與管理教授、全球水文監測聯盟主席 Albert van Dijk 表示：「這份報告再次強化了一項明確訊息：隨著地球升溫，水資源挑戰年復一年地升高。我們希望藉由提供變化與事件的資訊，支持知情決策，以在日益動盪的未來保護社區、基礎設施與生態系。」 ## 聯合國報告發現山區環境正以前所未有的速度改變 聯合國水資源機制（UN-Water）委由 UNESCO 發布的新報告《山脈與冰川──水塔》（Mountains and glaciers – Water towers）揭示，氣候擾動、生物多樣性流失與不可持續活動，正以前所未有的速度改變山區環境，威脅數十億人與無數生態系所依賴的水資源。 2025 年版《聯合國世界水資源發展報告》強調山地水源與高山冰川的重要性；它們對滿足基本人類供水與衛生需求至關重要。 這些水源也對居住在山區、周邊地區及下游區域的數十億人維持糧食與能源安全不可或缺，並透過各種依賴水資源的產業支持經濟成長。 報告指出，山脈是世界的「水塔」，在寒冷季節以冰雪形式儲存水，在溫暖季節釋放水，成為下游使用者的重要淡水來源。它們在全球水循環中扮演獨特而關鍵的角色，也影響大氣環流，進而驅動天氣與降水型態。 報告在凸顯氣候變遷對山地與冰川環境造成令人憂心影響的同時，也提出解決方案，以協助同時減緩並調適全球冰凍水資源的快速變化。報告清楚概述當前環境狀況，並提出必要的因應措施建議，包括迫切需要大幅減少碳排放。 透過說明山地淡水、基本服務與自然界之間的關聯，報告凸顯保育冰凍圈的重要性，以及其對永續發展目標（SDGs）可能造成的影響。 ## 洪災社區被發現具有長期健康風險 澳洲蒙納許大學研究人員進行的一項研究，被認為是全球規模最大、內容最完整的洪災長期健康影響研究。研究發現，洪災事件發生後長達七個月，社區住院風險增加 26%。 這項發表於《Nature Water》期刊的研究，分析 2010 至 2019 年間經歷重大洪災的八個國家──澳洲、越南、巴西、加拿大、智利、泰國、紐西蘭與台灣──747 個社區超過 3 億筆住院紀錄。研究區域包括澳洲新南威爾斯州東北部、亞馬遜河流域與巴西南部、越南湄公河流域及泰國南部。 研究發現，下列疾病的住院情形均增加：心血管疾病增加 35%、呼吸系統疾病增加 30%、傳染病增加 26%、消化系統疾病增加 30%、精神健康障礙增加 11%、糖尿病增加 61%、癌症增加 34%、神經系統疾病增加 34%，以及腎臟疾病增加 40%。 洪水的立即健康影響，例如溺水、觸電與失溫，本來即可預期；但這項研究發現，健康影響會延續較長時間，包括消化系統疾病風險升高，以及供水系統受到污染而導致感染擴散。 此外，研究指出，洪水可能造成有利於真菌、細菌、病毒及老鼠、昆蟲等病媒生長的環境，進而引發呼吸道、消化系統與傳染病的疫情。洪水也可能迫使大規模撤離，使人們流離失所、居住於臨時收容處，且只能使用有限衛生設施；衛生條件不佳會提高呼吸道、消化系統與傳染病的可能性。洪災後醫療服務的可近性與量能也可能受損，導致醫療介入延誤；財產損害與經濟損失帶來的心理壓力，則可能惡化或誘發不良健康結果。 補充此一圖像的是，美國哥倫比亞大學梅爾曼公共衛生學院的研究人員，與亞利桑那州立大學、哈佛大學及亞利桑那大學研究人員合作，於《Nature Medicine》發表研究，分析過去 20 年洪水對死亡率的影響。 研究人員運用統計模型，計算大型洪水發生後三個月期間的死亡率，與正常情況下相應期間的死亡率相比出現何種變化。 研究發現，熱帶風暴／颶風相關洪水發生當月，整體傷害死亡率增幅最大的是高齡者（24.9%）與女性（21.2%）。強降雨相關洪水則與傳染病死亡率增加 3.2%、心血管疾病死亡率增加 2.1% 有關；融雪相關洪水亦與某些疾病較高的死亡率有關。 ## 簡訊 ### 韌性：WMO 建立新夥伴關係 世界氣象組織（WMO）已與全球反飢餓與貧窮聯盟、綠色氣候基金準備計畫及 Santiago Network 簽署新協議。 面對日益增加的氣候與天氣相關挑戰，這些策略夥伴關係將提升 WMO 提供客製化氣候服務的能力、強化預警系統，並將水文氣象資料與專業知識整合到國家及國際決策架構中。 ### 韌性：古代地下水技術有助糧食安全 以色列巴伊蘭大學研究人員的一項新研究探討，古代農業做法如何有助於改善未來水與糧食安全。 這項發表於《Environmental Archaeology》的研究，探討以色列、伊朗、埃及、阿爾及利亞、加薩及伊比利半島大西洋沿岸海岸與內陸砂體中的傳統下凹式地下水採集農業生態系統。研究結合地理空間分析、考古發現與歷史文獻，說明早期伊斯蘭時期創新的集水與土壤肥沃化技術，如何改善今日農業系統。 ### 健康：英國首座污水監測試點 英國第一座污水監測試點中心已在巴斯大學成立。 「以水為基礎的健康保護早期預警系統卓越中心」研究人員將追蹤污水樣本中的病毒與細菌 DNA，為傳染病爆發提供早期預警系統。 ## 健康：WHO 發布新的 AMR 指引 世界衛生組織（WHO）發布出版品，提出《人類健康抗微生物藥物抗性研究議程》。 議程列出兩項與水有關的優先事項。 第一，是研究確保社區環境中安全管理的水、衛生與個人衛生（WASH）及廢棄物管理作法之介入措施，其影響、貢獻、實用性、效力與成本效益，以降低抗微生物藥物抗性。 第二，是研究醫療照護環境中 WASH 相關介入措施的執行策略，並評估其對抗藥性醫療照護相關感染，以及不同社會經濟情境下抗微生物藥物處方的影響、可接受性、公平性與成本效益。 ## 監測：水文學家掌握全球河川流量 美國麻薩諸塞大學阿默斯特分校與辛辛那提大學的研究人員，已在全球尺度繪製 35 年來河川變化的圖像。 這項發表於《Science》期刊的研究發現，河川下游流量顯著減少 44%，上游流量則增加 17%；這可能導致洪水影響，並擾亂生態系、水力發電開發與淡水供應。 研究人員使用衛星資料與電腦模型，掌握全世界 300 萬個河段的流量。 ## 技術：UNCCD 警告氣候造成的乾燥化危機 聯合國防治荒漠化公約（UNCCD）的一份新報告警告，近數十年來地球逾四分之三的陸地已永久變得更乾燥。 《乾燥土地的全球威脅：區域及全球乾燥化趨勢與未來預測》指出，與此前 30 年相比，截至 2020 年前的 30 年間，地球 77.6% 的陸地經歷較乾燥的狀況。 在此期間，乾地面積擴大約 430 萬平方公里──面積幾乎比印度大三分之一──涵蓋地球全部陸地（不含南極洲）的 40.6%。報告也發現，近數十年來全球 7.6% 的陸地──面積大於加拿大──跨越乾燥度門檻，例如從非乾地轉為乾地，或由較不乾燥的乾地類別轉為更乾燥的類別。大部分轉變區域原先是濕潤景觀，如今已成乾地；研究人員警告，若無法抑制溫室氣體排放，本世紀末全球另有 3% 的濕潤地區將變成乾地。 UNCCD 執行秘書 Ibrahim Thiaw 在發布報告時表示：「這項分析終於消除了長期以來籠罩全球乾燥化趨勢的任何不確定性。乾燥化危機首次以科學清晰度被記錄下來，揭露出影響全球數十億人的生存威脅。」 隨著地球持續升溫，報告最壞情境預測顯示，到本世紀末最多可能有 50 億人居住在乾地，對人類福祉與環境帶來嚴重影響。 報告作者提出因應乾燥化的完整路徑圖，強調減緩與調適，並建議強化乾燥化監測、改善土地使用做法、投資用水效率、建構韌性，以及建立國際合作更佳的框架。 ## 歐盟委員會提出水行動需求 歐盟委員會（EC）發布三份新報告，內容涵蓋歐洲水體狀況，以及保護和管理水資源所需採取的行動。 報告聚焦《水架構指令》、《洪水指令》與《海洋策略架構指令》的執行情況，指出過去六年的進展以及仍需加強之處。 除提供各國特定資訊與量身訂做的建議，協助持續推進歐洲永續水管理外，報告亦強調淡水與海洋水體狀況，提出改善水質與降低洪災風險的行動方向。 ## 超音波技術贏得月球水資源獎項 Naicker Scientific 開發的 SonoChem System 贏得 Aqualunar Challenge 英國賽道獎項。這項由英國太空總署委託辦理的 120 萬英鎊國際競賽，旨在推動用於月球探索的淨水技術創新。 SonoChem System 獲得 15 萬英鎊首獎，採用尖端超音波技術，在融化的月球冰中產生數百萬個微氣泡。這些微氣泡形成極端溫度與壓力，分解污染物，為太空人產生安全、可飲用的水。 研究團隊希望該系統未來也可開發為地球上的水處理應用。 ## WaterAid 發布新的 WASH 指引 WaterAid 發布新指引，說明將水、衛生與個人衛生（WASH）納入國家氣候計畫的關鍵要素。 2015 年《巴黎協定》提出一項行動計畫，協助各國調適日益增加的氣候變遷威脅。各國有兩項重要國家工具──國家自定貢獻（NDCs）與國家調適計畫（NAPs）──用以展現優先事項，並概述邁向調適與減緩目標的進展。 該出版品旨在支援各國於聯合國氣候變遷大會（COP30）前準備提出 NDC 與 NAP 更新；COP30 原訂於 11 月 10 至 21 日在巴西貝倫舉行。指引以五項準則，評估是否將具氣候韌性的 WASH 服務納入氣候政策： - 在制定 NDC 與 NAP 的過程中採取協商且具包容性的做法，並考量邊緣化或最脆弱群體的積極參與。 - 設定具性別回應的 WASH 目標與指標，優先照顧受潔淨水取得變化及不安全衛生設施影響最嚴重的婦女與女孩需求。 - 對 WASH 介入措施進行完整成本估算，反映所需資源的範圍及可能資金來源（國內或外部）。 - 跨部門整合 WASH 介入措施，反映具氣候韌性的 WASH 服務與系統所帶來的調適效益。 - 使 NDC 與 NAP 目標與「全球調適目標」的主題目標一致。 該指引旨在支援參與各國氣候政策、NAP 或 NDC 更新程序的人員，提供將 WASH 服務整合至 NAP 與 NDC 的基本指引。內容以 WaterAid 九個國家──孟加拉、布吉納法索、柬埔寨、衣索比亞、迦納、馬達加斯加、莫三比克、尼日與巴基斯坦──的 NAP 與 NDC 評估為基礎。 ## 發布美國氣候韌性水與衛生策略 太平洋研究所（Pacific Institute）與 DigDeep 發布一份新報告，為美國各地社區提供超過 100 項可採取的策略，以提供公平、具氣候韌性的供水與衛生服務。 報告題為《為前線社區實現公平且具氣候韌性的水與衛生服務》（Achieving Equitable, Climate-Resilient Water and Sanitation for Frontline Communities），是持續系列《美國的水、衛生與氣候變遷》的第三份報告。該系列由太平洋研究所、DigDeep 及水安全與合作中心（Center for Water Security and Cooperation）合作完成。 出版品辨識出實現公平、具氣候韌性水與衛生系統的八類特質與策略。這些面向指出系統性缺口，並提供透過下列方式推進水資源公平的可行路徑： - **建成基礎設施：** 報告發現，美國許多水系統已老舊，仍有數百萬人家中沒有自來水或管線。報告建議建置新的及改善既有的建成基礎設施解決方案，使其能承受極端氣候衝擊，確保服務公平且可靠。 - **技術與創新：** 報告指出，太陽能驅動供水系統等永續解決方案，已使納瓦霍族地區逾 750 戶家庭受益。報告建議引入能回應服務社區之社會、生態與政治脈絡的技術方法。 - **自然基礎設施：** 報告建議採用濕地復育與含水層補注等自然為本解決方案，改善水質及供水可靠度。 - **管理與規劃：** 報告建議採取以社區為中心的管理與規劃，用於水源保護、財務永續、災害整備、跨部門協調以及監測與評估。 - **資金與融資：** 報告指出，數十億美元聯邦資金結合地方、州政府與非政府組織資源，已促成調適，並支持氣候災害的整備、減緩與復原；但也警告未來聯邦資金機會存在不確定性，其他資金來源將是確保氣候韌性的關鍵。 - **知識與資訊：** 報告發現，透過包容性知識創造與分享方法，將地方及場域知識融入技術專業，可強化解決方案。這要求前線社區取得可使用且易於運用、符合其所在地及決策時程的水與氣候資料。 - **能力建構：** 報告建議投資人力發展、氣候素養訓練、建立社區與公用事業之間的信任，以及跨部門協調，使社區與公用事業具備因應未來挑戰的能力。 - **法律與政策：** 此框架包含法律與政策特質，但本報告尚未處理；系列下一份報告將辨識可增進前線社區飲用水系統氣候韌性的既有聯邦、州及地方法律與政策。

News Global study finds climate change is disrupting the Earth’s water cycle Anew report has found that global heating is changing the Earth’s water cycle - intensifying storms, floods and droughts, affecting entire ecosystems and billions of people. The ‘2024 Global Water Monitor Report’, produced by an international team of researchers from universities in Australia, Saudi Arabia, China, Germany and elsewhere, used data from thousands of ground stations and satellites to assess critical water variables such as rainfall, soil moisture, river flows, and flooding. The researchers warn that global heating is changing the way water moves around the Earth, with water disasters in 2024 - the hottest year on record - driving 40 million people from their homes and causing economic damage of more than $550bn. Rising temperatures caused by the continued use of fossil fuels can disrupt the water cycle in multiple ways, with warmer air holding more water vapour, leading to more intense downpours; warmer seas providing more energy to hurricanes and typhoons; and global heating increasing drought because of greater evaporation of water from soil, in addition to disrupting rainfall patterns. The report’s authors warn that seasonal climate forecasts for 2025, assessed alongside current environmental conditions, suggest droughts could worsen in northern South America, southern Africa, and parts of Asia, with wetter regions such as Sahel (a biogeographical region in Africa) and Europe potentially exposed to elevated flood risk. This third annual report builds on the work of previous years, summarising the state of the global water cycle in 2024, identifying key trends and analysing major hydrological events. Albert van Dijk, Professor of Water Science and Management at the Australian National University, and Chair of the Global Water Monitor Consortium, said: “This report reinforces a clear message: as the planet warms, water challenges are escalating, year after year. Flooded communities found to have long-term health risks A study by researchers from Monash University, Australia - believed to be the world’s largest and most comprehensive study of the long-term health impacts of flooding - has found that communities face a 26% increase in the risk of hospitalisation up to seven months after a flood event. The study, published in the journal Nature Water, analysed more than 300 million hospitalisation records from 747 communities in eight countries (Australia, Vietnam, Brazil, Canada, Chile, Thailand, New Zealand and Taiwan) that had experienced major flood events between the period of 2010 and 2019. Regions included the northeast region of New South Wales, Australia, the Amazon River and the southern region of Brazil, the Mekong Basin in Vietnam, and the southern region of Thailand. The study found increases in hospitalisation for cardiovascular diseases (35%), respiratory diseases (30%), infectious diseases (26%), digestive diseases (30%), mental health disorders (11%), diabetes (61%), cancer (34%), nervous system disorders (34%), and renal diseases (40%). While immediate health impacts of flooding, such as drowning, electrocution and hypothermia, would be expected, this study found long-term impacts on health, including an elevated risk of digestive diseases and the spread of infection through the contamination of water supply systems. Additionally, the study highlights that floods can create environments that are conducive to the growth of fungi, bacteria, viruses, and vectors such as mice and insects, which can trigger outbreaks of respiratory, digestive and infectious diseases, and force mass evacuations, causing displacement, with people living in temporary shelters and having access to limited sanitation facilities - resulting in poor hygiene, with conditions raising the likelihood of respiratory, digestive, and infectious diseases. Access and capacity to healthcare services may also be impaired after floods, leading to delays in medical interventions; and psychological stress from property damage and financial losses can worsen or induce adverse health outcomes. Adding to this picture, a study by researchers from Columbia University’s Mailman School of Public Health, in partnership with researchers at Arizona State University, Harvard University and the University of Arizona, USA, published in Nature Medicine, analysed the impact of floods on mortality over the past 20 years. The researchers used a statistical model to calculate how death rates changed in three-month periods following large floods compared with equivalent periods in normal conditions. The researchers found the largest overall increases in injury death rates among older people (24.9%) and females (21.2%) during the month of tropical storm/hurricane-related flooding, with increases in death rates associated with heavy rain-related flooding for infectious diseases (3.2%) and cardiovascular diseases (2.1%). Snowmelt-related floods were also associated with higher death rates for certain diseases. Technology UNCCD warns of climate-induced aridity crisis Anew report by the UN Convention to Combat Desertification (UNCCD) warns that more than three-quarters of the land on Earth has become permanently drier in recent decades. ‘The Global Threat of Drying Lands: Regional and global aridity trends and future projections’ states that 77.6% of Earth’s land experienced drier conditions during the three decades leading up to 2020 compared with the previous 30-year period. Over this period, drylands expanded by about 4.3 million km? - an area nearly a third larger than India - covering 40.6% of all land on Earth (excluding Antarctica). It also finds that in recent decades 7.6% of global land - an area larger than Canada - was pushed across aridity thresholds (i.¢., from non-drylands to drylands, or from less arid dryland classes to more arid classes). Most of the areas that have transitioned were previously humid landscapes that have now become drylands, with researchers warning that failure to curb greenhouse gas emissions would result in another 3% of the world’s humid areas becoming drylands by the end of this century. Launching the report, UNCCD Executive Secretary Ibrahim Thiaw said: “This analysis finally dispels any uncertainty that has long surrounded global drying trends. For the first time, the aridity crisis has been documented with scientific clarity, revealing an existential threat affecting billions around the globe.” As the planet continues to warm, the report’s worst-case scenario projections suggest up to 5 billion people could live in drylands by the end of the century, with dire implications for the wellbeing of people and the environment. The report’s authors provide a comprehensive roadmap for tackling aridity, emphasising mitigation and adaptation, and recommending a strengthening of aridity monitoring, improved land use practices, investments in water efficiency, resilience building, and the development of international frameworks for improved cooperation. Ultrasonic technology wins lunar water prize The SonoChem System developed by Naicker Scientific has won the UK track of the Aqualunar Challenge, delivered on behalf of the UK Space Agency. The £1.2 million international competition was created to drive innovation in water purification technologies for lunar exploration. Awarded the grand prize of £150,000, the SonoChem System employs cutting-edge ultrasonic technology to generate millions of microbubbles in melted lunar ice. These microbubbles create extreme temperatures and pressures that break down contaminants, producing safe, drinkable water for astronauts. It is hoped that the system may also be developed for terrestrial water treatment applications. WaterAid publishes new WASH guidance WaterAid has published new guidance outlining key factors for the integration of water, sanitation and hygiene (WASH) into national climate plans. In 2015, the Paris Agreement laid out an action plan for countries to adapt to growing threats from climate change. Countries have two key national instruments - Nationally Determined Contributions (NDCs) and National Adaptation Plans (NAPs) - to demonstrate their priorities and outline progress towards their adaptation and mitigation targets. Intended to support countries as they prepare to present updates to their NDCs and NAPs ahead of the United Nations (UN) Climate Change Conference (COP30), to be held on 10-21 November in Belém, Brazil, the publication provides guidance to assess the integration of climate resilient WASH services into climate policies using five criteria: © Aconsultative and inclusive NDC and NAP development process that considers the active participation of those who are marginalised or most vulnerable. Gender-responsive WASH targets and indicators that prioritise the needs of women and girls who are worst affected by changes in the availability of clean water and by unsafe sanitation facilities. Fully costed WASH interventions that reflect the extent of the resources required and potential sources of funding (domestic or external). The integration of WASH interventions across sectors, reflecting the adaptation benefits of climate resilient WASH services and systems. NDC and NAP targets that are aligned with the ‘Global Goal on Adaptation’ thematic targets. The guidance is aimed to support those involved in their country’s process for updating climate policies, NAPs or NDCs. It provides basic guidance on how to integrate WASH services in NAPs and NDCs, and is based on the assessment of NAPs and NDCs in nine WaterAid countries: Bangladesh, Burkina Faso, Cambodia, Ethiopia, Ghana, Madagascar, Mozambique, Niger and Pakistan. Strategy for US climate resilient water and sanitation published A new report by the Pacific Institute and DigDeep outlines more than 100 actionable strategies for the delivery of equitable, climate resilient, water and sanitation services to communities across the USA. The report, titled ‘Achieving Equitable, Climate-Resilient Water and Sanitation for Frontline Communities’, is the third in the ongoing series ‘Water, Sanitation and Climate Change in the United States’, which is the result of a partnership between the Pacific Institute, DigDeep, and the Center for Water Security and Cooperation. The publication identifies eight categories of attributes and strategies for achieving equitable, climate-resilient water and sanitation systems. These areas highlight systemic gaps and provide actionable pathways for advancing water equity through: e@ Built infrastructure: The report finds that many US water systems are outdated, and millions of people still lack running water or plumbing in their homes. It recommends the implementation of new and improved built infrastructure solutions that can withstand extreme climate impacts to ensure equitable, reliable services. @ Technology and innovation: The report highlights that sustainable solutions, such as solar-powered water systems, have already benefitted more than 750 households in the Navajo Nation. It recommends the introduction of technological approaches that respond to the social, ecological, and political contexts of the communities they serve. Natural infrastructure: The report recommends the adoption of nature-based solutions, such as wetland restoration and aquifer recharge, to improve water quality and the reliability of water supplies. Management and planning: The report recommends community-centred management and planning for source water protection, financial sustainability, disaster preparedness, cross-sectoral coordination, and monitoring and evaluation. Funding and financing: The report states that billions of dollars in federal funding, combined with local, state and NGO sources, have spurred adaptation and supported climate disaster preparedness, mitigation and recovery. However, it warns that the future of federal funding opportunities is uncertain and says other funding sources will be critical to ensure climate resilience. Knowledge and information: The report finds that solutions are strengthened by incorporating local and place-based knowledge with technical expertise through inclusive approaches to knowledge creation and sharing. This requires frontline communities to have accessible and usable water and climate data that is specific to their location and decision-making timeframes. Capacity building: The report recommends investments in workforce development, climate literacy training, trust-building between communities and utilities, and cross-sector coordination to equip communities and utilities to meet future challenges. Law and policy: While the framework includes law and policy attributes, this report does not. The next report in this series will identify existing federal, state and local laws and policies that enhance the climate resilience of drinking water systems for frontline communities.
