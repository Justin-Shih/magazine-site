# IWA 新聞 ## 泰國與印尼成為理事會員 IWA 很高興宣布，泰國與印尼已以理事會員（Governing Members，GMs）身分加入本協會。 為推進「水智慧世界」的願景，理事會員是 IWA 在國家與區域層級推動工作的重要力量。理事會員提供以國家為核心的連結，召集當地人士交流最佳實務、探索水務解決方案的創新方式，培育並擴展 IWA 網絡，並分享水務領域最新的科學、技術與最佳實務。 IWA 的 70 個理事會員反映各國水務部門的多元性，也映照出 IWA 全球網絡的廣度與實力。他們透過串聯不同部門與專業領域，強化水務產業，進而在第一線的水政策、水務實作與水資源管理上促成實質改變。 理事會員由來自水務各領域代表所組成的委員會帶領，並在 IWA 理事大會中共同集會。他們也可使用 Connect Plus 平台，在工作上獲得支援，並促進個人會員之間的互動。 代表泰國理事會員的泰國環境工程學會（EEAT）主席 Suchat Leungprasert 教授表示：「目前，水資源管理在泰國是一項重大議題，也愈來愈受到泰國政府重視。因此，專業知識與技術對改善國內水資源管理的推動相當重要。 「EEAT 的研究人員與會員可進行各項研究工作，包括農業、工業與社區用途的廢水處理和水資源管理。此外，EEAT 與泰國政府及數所享有盛譽的大學已建立關係。我們可為泰國及鄰近地區會員的 IWA 活動提供很好的據點。」 印尼飲用水企業協會（PERPAMSI，印尼水協會）主席 Arief Wisnu Cahyono 表示：「我們很自豪成為 IWA 理事會員，因為這強化了我們透過全球合作推進水資源管理的承諾。這項會員資格提供寶貴的知識交流、創新與能力建構機會；這些機會對支持印尼乃至更廣泛地區的永續供水服務至關重要。」 ## 發展動態：IWA 參與 AfWASA 大會暨展覽 IWA 很高興參與非洲水與衛生協會（AfWASA）第 22 屆國際大會暨展覽（International Congress & Exhibition 2025）。活動於 2 月 16 日至 20 日在烏干達坎帕拉舉行。 AfWASA ICE 2025 以「全民享有水與衛生：非洲的安全未來」為主題，致力於在聯合國永續發展目標（SDG）2030 年期限前，推進非洲水與衛生挑戰的應對工作。 IWA 與 AfWASA 長期合作，且在非洲大陸的理事會員與青年水專業人士（Young Water Professionals，YWP）分會數量持續增加。 ## 數位水計畫委員會的新成員 IWA 數位水計畫指導委員會迎來五位新成員。 為強化其專業能力與全球觸及範圍，新委員會納入來自公用事業、解決方案供應商、顧問及資訊與通訊技術領域的專業人士。 2025 至 2027 年委員會展現 IWA 的多元、平等與共融（DEI）標準：女性代表占 45%，來自低收入與中等收入國家（LMICs）的成員占 40%。 3 月 14 日，卸任與新任委員舉行正式交接會議，討論計畫目標並集思廣益未來行動方向。 IWA 執行長 Kala Vairavamoorthy 感謝卸任委員，並鼓勵新團隊帶來嶄新觀點、聚焦新興技術，同時強化 IWA 在數位水領域的角色。 指導委員會主席 Oliver Grievson 也呼應上述觀點，敦促委員辨識產業挑戰、持續與部門轉型保持一致，並運用全球案例研究來創造影響。 卸任委員 Saba Daneshgar 分享自己在指導委員會的歷程：他以青年水專業人士身分加入，並有機會與多元專家團隊合作多項計畫。他也指出，自己在委員會任職期間體會到，學界與實務工作者從不同視角理解數位化，突顯跨部門溝通的重要性。 2025 至 2027 年指導委員會將引導數位水計畫進入下一階段，確保數位化持續成為永續水資源管理的核心。

Thailand and Indonesia become Governing Members IWA is delighted that Thailand and Indonesia have joined the Association as Governing Members (GMs). At the heart of IWA’s efforts to advance a vision of a water wise world, GMs are vital to WA's work at national and regional level. The GMs provide a country focus, convening people locally to share best practice and explore new ways of innovating water solutions, nurturing and growing the |WA network, and sharing the latest science, technology and best practice from across the sector. IWA’s 70 GMs reflect the diversity of the water sector at country level, mirroring the breadth and strength of IWA’s global network. They strengthen the water industry by bridging divides between sectors and disciplines to create real change in water policy, water practice and water management on the ground. Led by committees of representatives from across the water sector, GMs importantly convene for the |WA Governing Assembly and are supported in their work by access to the Connect Plus platform, facilitating interaction among individual members. Prof Suchat Leungprasert, President of the Environmental Engineering Association of Thailand (EEAT), representing the Thailand GM, said: “Currently, water resource management in Thailand is a big issue and is gaining interest from the Thai government. As a result, expertise and technologies are important in the drive to improve water management in the country. “Researchers and members of the EEAT are available to perform research-related activities, including wastewater treatment and water resource management for agricultural, industrial and community uses. In addition, EEAT has an established relationship with the Thai government and several highly regarded universities in Thailand. We provide a great location for WA activities for members in and near to Thailand.” Arief Wisnu Cahyono, Chair of Persatuan Perusahaan Air Minum Seluruh Indonesia (PERPAMSI), the Indonesian Water Association, said: “We are proud to be an WA Governing Member as it strengthens our commitment to global collaboration in advancing water management. This membership provides valuable opportunities for knowledge exchange, innovation, and capacity building, which are essential for supporting sustainable water services in Indonesia and beyond.” Development IWA at AfWASA Congress & Exhibition IWA was pleased to participate in the African Water and Sanitation Association’s (AfWASA) 22nd International Congress & Exhibition 2025, which took place on 16-20 February in Kampala, Uganda. Under the theme of ‘Water and Sanitation for All: A Secure Future for Africa’, AfWASA ICE 2025 worked to advance progress on tackling Africa’s water and sanitation challenges ahead of the UN Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 2030 deadline. IWA has enjoyed long cooperation with AfWASA and has a growing number of Governing Members and Young Water Professionals (YWP) Chapters across the continent. New faces on Digital Water Programme committee IWA’s Digital Water Programme Steering Committee has welcomed five new members. Strengthening its expertise and global reach, the new group includes professionals from utilities, solution providers, consultants, and information and communication technology sectors. The 2025-2027 Committee reflects IWA’s Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) standards, with 45% female representation and 40% from low-and middle-income countries (LMICs). On 14 March, outgoing and incoming members met for the official handover meeting to discuss the objectives of the programme and brainstorm future actions. IWA Executive Director Kala Vairavamoorthy thanked the outgoing members and encouraged the new group to bring fresh perspectives, while shining a light on emerging technologies, as well as strengthening IWA’s role in digital water. Steering Committee Chair Oliver Grievson echoed these points and urged members to identify industry challenges, stay aligned with sector transformations, and use global case studies to drive impact. Outgoing member Saba Daneshgar shared his journey in the Steering Committee, joining as a Young Water Professional (YWP) and having the opportunity to work with a diverse group of experts on multiple projects. He also noted that his tenure on the Committee showed him that academia and practitioners view digitalisation through different lenses, highlighting the importance of communication across sectors. The 2025-2027 Steering Committee is set to guide the Digital Water Programme into its next phase, ensuring digitalisation remains central to sustainable water management.
