Thailand and Indonesia become Governing Members IWA is delighted that Thailand and Indonesia have joined the Association as Governing Members (GMs). At the heart of IWA’s efforts to advance a vision of a water wise world, GMs are vital to WA's work at national and regional level. The GMs provide a country focus, convening people locally to share best practice and explore new ways of innovating water solutions, nurturing and growing the |WA network, and sharing the latest science, technology and best practice from across the sector. IWA’s 70 GMs reflect the diversity of the water sector at country level, mirroring the breadth and strength of IWA’s global network. They strengthen the water industry by bridging divides between sectors and disciplines to create real change in water policy, water practice and water management on the ground. Led by committees of representatives from across the water sector, GMs importantly convene for the |WA Governing Assembly and are supported in their work by access to the Connect Plus platform, facilitating interaction among individual members. Prof Suchat Leungprasert, President of the Environmental Engineering Association of Thailand (EEAT), representing the Thailand GM, said: “Currently, water resource management in Thailand is a big issue and is gaining interest from the Thai government. As a result, expertise and technologies are important in the drive to improve water management in the country. “Researchers and members of the EEAT are available to perform research- related activities, including wastewater treatment and water resource management for agricultural, industrial and community uses. In addition, EEAT has an established relationship with the Thai government and several highly regarded universities in Thailand. We provide a great location for WA activities for members in and near to Thailand.” Arief Wisnu Cahyono, Chair of Persatuan Perusahaan Air Minum Seluruh Indonesia (PERPAMSI), the Indonesian Water Association, said: “We are proud to be an WA Governing Member as it strengthens our commitment to global collaboration in advancing water management. This membership provides valuable opportunities for knowledge exchange, innovation, and capacity building, which are essential for supporting sustainable water services in Indonesia and beyond.” Development IWA at AfWASA Congress & Exhibition IWA was pleased to participate in the African Water and Sanitation Association’s (AfWASA) 22nd International Congress & Exhibition 2025, which took place on 16-20 February in Kampala, Uganda. Under the theme of ‘Water and Sanitation for All: A Secure Future for Africa’, AfWASA ICE 2025 worked to advance progress on tackling Africa’s water and sanitation challenges ahead of the UN Sustainable Development Goal (SDG) 2030 deadline. IWA has enjoyed long cooperation with AfWASA and has a growing number of Governing Members and Young Water Professionals (YWP) Chapters across the continent. 12 | The Source | April 2025 New faces on Digital Water Programme committee IWA’s Digital Water Programme Steering Committee has welcomed five new members. Strengthening its expertise and global reach, the new group includes professionals from utilities, solution providers, consultants, and information and communication technology sectors. The 2025-2027 Committee reflects IWA’s Diversity, Equity, and Inclusion (DEI) standards, with 45% female representation and 40% from low- and middle-income countries (LMICs). On 14 March, outgoing and incoming members met for the official handover meeting to discuss the objectives of the programme and brainstorm future actions. IWA Executive Director Kala Vairavamoorthy thanked the outgoing members and encouraged the new group to bring fresh perspectives, while shining a light on emerging technologies, as well as strengthening IWA’s role in digital water. Steering Committee Chair Oliver Grievson echoed these points and urged members to identify industry challenges, stay aligned with sector transformations, and use global case studies to drive impact. Outgoing member Saba Daneshgar shared his journey in the Steering Committee, joining as a Young Water Professional (YWP) and having the opportunity to work with a diverse group of experts on multiple projects. He also noted that his tenure on the Committee showed him that academia and practitioners view digitalisation through different lenses, highlighting the importance of communication across sectors. The 2025-2027 Steering Committee is set to guide the Digital Water Programme into its next phase, ensuring digitalisation remains central to sustainable water management.
