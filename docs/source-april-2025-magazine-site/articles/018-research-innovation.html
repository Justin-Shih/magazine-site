# 研究與創新 ## 處理：研究人員探究石墨烯用於水過濾的潛力 德國維爾茨堡朱利葉斯─馬克西米利安大學（JMU）有機化學研究所與奈米系統化學中心的研究人員，研究石墨烯的離子滲透性，目標是開發新的水過濾產品。 一個化學家團隊已成功透過在雙層奈米石墨烯系統中刻意製造缺陷，控制鹵化物離子的通過。研究結果發表於《Nature》期刊，顯示可應用於水過濾或感測技術的新潛力。 石墨烯是一種由純碳構成、極薄、可撓且耐用的材料。它形成的薄層幾乎只由單層碳原子組成；若要使石墨烯達到人類頭髮的厚度，必須將數千層這樣的薄層堆疊起來。 科學家關注的是：在材料的碳晶格製造「缺陷」，能否控制石墨烯對不同物質的滲透性。JMU 的 Frank Würthner 教授解釋：「可把這些缺陷想成使晶格能讓氣體通過的小孔。」然而，過去尚未觀察到氟化物、氯化物或溴化物等離子對其他物質的滲透性。他指出，這對海水淡化及混合物偵測或純化等應用，具有基礎科學意義。 Würthner 帶領的團隊首次建立了一套具有缺陷的模型系統，能讓氟化物、氯化物與溴化物通過，但不讓碘化物通過。這項成果是以兩片奈米石墨烯組成、包圍一個空腔的穩定雙層結構達成。 穿透的鹵化物離子會在這個空腔中結合，因此可量測其進入所需時間。 與 Würthner 共同發起並領導計畫的 Kazutaka Shoyama 博士表示：「單層奈米石墨烯對氯化物具有高滲透性的證明，以及雙層奈米石墨烯對鹵化物的選擇性結合，使一些應用更接近實現。」這些應用包括水過濾膜、人工受體與氯離子通道。 團隊計畫建構更大的奈米石墨烯堆疊，進一步研究離子的流動。 ## 資源：美國計畫辨識浮動式太陽能潛力 一項由美國能源部能源效率與再生能源辦公室下屬太陽能技術辦公室及水力技術辦公室資助的新研究顯示，由聯邦政府管理的水庫可設置浮動式太陽能板，發電量潛力約可供 1 億戶美國家庭使用。 這項發表於《Solar Energy》的研究，被認為是首次探討哪些水體具備支持此類發電廠適當條件的研究。研究納入航運交通的考量，因船隻造成的尾流可能損壞繫泊線並影響浮動基礎設施；其他條件包括水體過冷，以及水庫過淺或底部坡度過陡、無法穩固固定太陽能板。 研究辨識出一些水力發電水庫可能是浮動式太陽能發電廠的理想場址，可形成同時運用太陽能與水力發電的混合能源系統，為電網提供更可靠、更具韌性的能源。 計畫未來將進行檢視，辨識哪些水庫鄰近輸電線路、當地用電需求、特定場址開發的可能成本、環境考量，以及開發商可能如何因應州與聯邦法規。團隊也希望評估更多潛在地點，包括較小型水庫、河口，甚至海洋場址。 ## 研究與創新：美國環境安全計畫測試新的 PFAS 技術 Revive Environmental Technology（Revive Environmental）與 Battelle 獲選參與一項美國國防部環境安全技術認證計畫；此計畫透過國防創新單位（Defense Innovation Unit）簽約執行。 該計畫目標是示範一項首創技術，可破壞有毒的全氟及多氟烷基物質（PFAS），從而消除其對環境的污染。現地示範將在美國底特律一處 Clean Earth 危險廢棄物服務設施，展示 Revive Environmental 的 PFAS Annihilator 技術。

Research & Innovation Treatment Researchers study graphene’s potential for water filtration Researchers at the Institute of Organic Chemistry and the Center for Nanosystems Chemistry, Julius-Maximilians-Universitat (JMU), University of Wiirzburg, Germany, have studied the ion permeability of graphene with the aim of developing new products for water filtration. A team of chemists has succeeded in controlling the passage of halide ions by deliberately introducing defects into a two-layer nanographene system. Their results, published in the journal Nature, suggest new potential applications in water filtration or sensor technology. Graphene is an extremely thin, flexible and resistant material made of pure carbon. It forms layers that consist of virtually a single layer of carbon atoms. To make graphene as thick as a human hair, thousands of such layers would have to be stacked on top of each other. Scientists are interested in the benefits of controlling the permeability of graphene for different substances by creating ‘defects’ in the carbon lattice of the material. “These can be thought of as small holes that make the lattice permeable to gases,” explains Professor Frank Wiirthner, from JMU. Permeability to other substances, such as ions like fluoride, chloride or bromide, had not been observed. However, he explains that this would be of fundamental scientific interest for applications such as the desalination of water and the detection or purification of mixtures of substances. For the first time, a team led by Wirthner has now created a model system with a defect that allows the halides fluoride, chloride and bromide to pass through, but not iodide. This was achieved ina stable double layer consisting of two nanographenes that encloses a cavity. The penetrated halide ions are bound in this cavity so that the time required for entry can be measured. “The proof of a high permeability for chloride by single-layer nanographene and a selective binding of halides in a double-layer of nanographene brings some applications closer,” says Dr Kazutaka Shoyama, who initiated and led the project together with Wiirthner. Such applications include water filtration membranes, artificial receptors and chloride channels. Other considerations include waterbodies that are too cold and reservoirs that are too shallow or have sloping bottoms that are too steep to secure solar panels in place. The study identifies some hydropower reservoirs that could be ideal locations for floating solar power plants, creating a hybrid energy system that uses both solar energy and hydropower to provide a more reliable and resilient energy source to the power grid. Future plans for the project include a review to identify which reservoirs are located close to transmission lines, the local electricity demands, the potential costs for development at specific sites, environmental considerations, and how developers might navigate state and federal regulations. The team would also like to evaluate more potential locations, including smaller reservoirs, estuaries, and even ocean sites. Research & Innovation US Environmental Security programme tests new PFAS technology Revive Environmental Technology (Revive Environmental) and Battelle have been selected to participate in a Department of Defense Environmental Security Technology Certification Program project contracted through the Defense Innovation Unit, USA. The programme aims to demonstrate a first-of-its-kind technology capable of destroying toxic per-and polyfluoroalky! substances (PFAS), eliminating pollution to the environment. The field demonstration will showcase Revive Environmental’s PFAS Annihilator at a Clean Earth hazardous waste service facility, in Detroit, USA.
