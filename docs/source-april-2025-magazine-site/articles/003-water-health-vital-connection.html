中文全文翻譯

# 水與健康：至關重要的連結 我們水務領域與聯合國永續發展目標（SDGs）之間，有一種不尋常的關係。我們一再指出與水相關的永續發展目標 6（SDG 6）尚未被滿足的需求，本應能引起重視，並促使各方回應這項特定目標所面臨的問題。然而，當實際回應未達所需程度時，我們也可以指出它與其他多項永續發展目標之間更廣泛的連結──部分是希望協助推進那些目標，部分也是希望藉由這些連結，為「我們的」SDG 6 爭取進展。 若過度鋪陳這些連結，反而可能削弱水議題本身的論點；原本表達得很好的主張，只要顯露任何薄弱處，便可能顯得不夠令人信服。 不過，確實有些發展面向是彼此並行、相互關聯的。水與健康之間至關重要的連結便是一例。本期數篇新聞文章都凸顯：證明這項連結重要性的證據，至今仍在持續累積。 其中一例是抗微生物藥物抗性（AMR）議題。世界衛生組織（WHO）最近已就此發布研究議程（見「新聞與分析」）。 這份出版品的核心，是 WHO 正在推動的一組建議研究優先事項。其中包括：更完整地了解水、衛生與個人衛生（WASH）如何有助於減少抗生素的使用需求，以及醫療照護設施內 WASH 的實施策略。（見「分析」。） WHO 強調的研究優先事項，也涵蓋更廣泛的跨領域議題；例如，需要更深入理解取得水與衛生服務受限，可能如何導致感染具 AMR 的病原體。 水與健康連結的另一個面向，是近期研究揭示了洪災對人類健康的影響（見「新聞」）。溺水到觸電等直接影響，兩者的關聯在直覺上十分清楚；但這些研究採取的視角，比直接影響更為廣泛。 一項被認為是全球規模最大、內容最完整的洪災長期健康影響研究，檢視了全球八個國家近 750 個社區在 20 年期間內，洪災後的住院情形。研究除整體觀察外，並分析 10 種特定健康問題，因而發現更廣泛健康影響的證據。 另一項研究則探討過去 20 年洪災對死亡率的影響，同樣顯示出更廣泛影響的證據。 面對氣候變遷可能使洪災更為劇烈的憂慮，這類研究指出：除了較直接的影響外，水與健康這類連結正變得愈加重要。 隨著水與健康連結所涵蓋的關切面向擴大，去年的 IWA 全球水獎頒給 Joan Rose 教授便格外恰當。本期第 25 頁分享了她的觀點。她特別指出，在水環境承受更廣泛水質壓力之際，來自人類與動物、並且可能持續增加的全球微生物負荷，值得關注。 她的專業與熱忱，反映出 IWA 內部水與健康之間深厚的連結；這不僅展現在我們的專業小組，也展現在以這兩項交織主題為核心的期刊。很明顯地，這是一項深刻的連結，未來數年將具有極高的重要性。 Keith Hayward，編者

英文官方網頁原文

The Source官方網頁原文（查詢日期：2026-09-10）

We in the water sector have an unusual relationship with the UN Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Our highlighting of the unmet needs around water-related SDG 6 ought to hit home and prompt a response to tackle the concerns of this particular goal. Faced, though, with a response that falls short, we can point to multiple wider connections across other SDGs – partly in the hope of helping advance those goals, but partly also in the hope of leveraging these connections to secure progress on ‘our’ SDG 6. Overplaying the connections risks undermining the case for water – an otherwise well-put argument can seem less than convincing if any weaknesses are apparent. At the same time, there are aspects of development that do go hand in hand. The vital connection between water and health is one such example. Several of the news articles in this edition underline how evidence of the importance of this connection is still emerging. One example is the issue of antimicrobial resistance (AMR), with the World Health Organization (WHO) recently having published its research agenda on this theme (see News and Analysis). At the core of the publication is a set of recommended research priorities that WHO has been working to advance. It includes fuller understanding of the contribution of WASH to, for example, reducing the need for antibiotic use, and strategies for WASH in health-care facilities. (See Analysis). The research priorities that WHO highlights also include wider cross-cutting concerns, such as the need to gain a better understanding of how, for example, limited access to water and sanitation might contribute to infection with AMR. In another dimension of the water-health connection, recent research has shed light on the impacts of flooding on human health (see News). The connection with direct impacts, from drowning to electrocution, is intuitively clear. However, this research has taken a broader approach than this. One study, believed to be the world’s largest and most comprehensive of the long-term health impacts of flooding, looked at post-flood hospitalisations over a 20-year period for almost 750 communities in eight countries around the world. Looking overall and at 10 specific health issues, evidence of wider health impacts emerged. Another study looked at the impact of floods on death rates over the last 20 years, again revealing evidence of wider impacts. Faced with concerns around the prospects for more intense flooding due to climate change, such research points to growing relevance of this type of water-health connection on top of the more direct impacts. Given such widening of the concerns around water-health connections, it is fitting that the recipient of last year’s IWA Global Water Award was Professor Joan Rose. Her views are shared in this issue (p25). She highlights in particular the prospect of a growing global microbial load – of both human and animal origin – on the water environment, as part of wider pressures on water quality. Her expertise and passion reflect a deep connection within IWA between water and health – seen not least in our Specialist Group and our journal on these entwined themes. It is clear that this is a deep connection that will be of huge relevance in the years ahead. Keith Hayward, Editor
