以開放平台塑造水與衛生的未來
An open platform shaping the future

會長專欄 / The President

← 返回目錄 / Back to contents

中文完整摘要說明

IWA 會長回顧協會如何透過實體大會、網路研討會、專業社群與治理會員，連結個人及組織，讓國際合作、科學知識與實務經驗互相流動。曼谷水與發展大會顯示，開放召集平台能讓多個議程、夥伴與專業世代共同推進水與衛生工作。

中文全文翻譯

作者指出，IWA 長期提供個人成長與國際合作的雙向平台。會員從全球關係與觀點中獲得專業養分，同時也把自己的經驗帶回協會，共同改善水與衛生管理、公共健康、城市安全、環境與生物多樣性。 2025 年曼谷 Water and Development Congress & Exhibition 是這種開放平台的例子。大會由 Asian Institute of Technology 共同主辦，並與 Asian Development Bank、World Bank、South Africa Water Research Commission 等夥伴合作，透過主題演講、高階峰會與專門論壇推進多項議程。IWA 的網路研討會、專業小組及 Governing Membership 也以相同方式連結研究、產業、公共部門與國際組織。 文章的核心主張是，水與衛生的複雜問題需要能讓不同角色坦誠交流、建立關係並共同試驗的場域。IWA 不直接取代各地機關與公用事業，而是透過召集、知識交換與合作協議，提高專業社群形成共同行動的能力。

Original Full Text

iw As Ihave written before, IWA has been central to my career. As a longstanding member, I can look back personally at the relationships that have been forged and the international perspectives that have been shared. These have helped inform and shape my view of the world, and through these interactions have come opportunities for collaboration. This is why I have always encouraged young water professionals to join IWA — because as a global organisation focused on water and attracting the brightest and best from around the world, it is uniquely positioned to leverage opportunities and accelerate advancements in the profession. For individuals, the relationship with IWA is two-way. It offers an opportunity for personal growth while, at the same time, individuals contribute through IWA to international collaboration that has the capacity to transform the management of water and sanitation, shaping lives and livelihoods, improving health, safeguarding cities, supporting the environment and biodiversity, and creating places where people can thrive. At a broader level, IWA provides a platform for connecting not just individuals but also the organisations they represent. This is very visible in particular with IWA’s events, many of which I have had great pleasure of taking part in. Our Congresses and wider event portfolio provide a platform for the very best minds in the sector, creating opportunities for international experts to engage and share knowledge, a place where leading organisations in the sector can launch plans for future advancements based on leading science to an informed, receptive audience. Such opportunities are precious, and IWA has excelled in creating this environment. Through our platform we offer places where people and the organisations they represent can convene, discuss, forge relationships — all with the intention of advancing the profession and the global community that depends on us. The 2025 Water and Development Congress & Exhibition just held in Bangkok is a perfect example. The event shared insights for advancing pathways to progress and a resilient future, with contributions from right across our sector. As an open platform, it brought together partners and participants in a format where the connecting webs of collaboration have the potential for lasting positive impact. Our lead partners included co- organiser Asian Institute of Technology, the Asian Development Bank as Principal Strategic Partner, and the World Bank and South Africa’s Water Research Commission as Strategic Partners, as well as other notable local partners. It means that Bangkok, with its array of programme features, keynotes, high-level summit and dedicated forums on key themes, provided a place where multiple agendas were advanced. This highlights IWA’s value of operating as an open convening platform. We saw the same approach at work at our last World Water Congress & Exhibition, held in Toronto, Canada, which saw, for example, the participation of the leadership of numerous key industry organisations from around the world. We can see a similar approach in other aspects of our activities. Many of you will, for example, have tuned into one of IWA’s many webinars. Just like our physical events, these provide an opportunity to engage with industry leaders and discuss key subjects that are essential to the progress of the sector. A good number of these IWA webinars are staged with other organisations, again reflecting our ethos of working as a collaborative open platform. So, partners on our webinars have included The Nature Conservancy, the World Bank, IBNET, AfWASA, Blue Peace Middle East, and IUCN, as examples. This all gives a flavour of the rich web of relationships that our network helps sustain. It is a web that runs deep and wide. It adds power to our programmatic work. And it spans loose cooperations through to formalised partnership agreements — including the important sector organisational buy-in that is evident in our Governing Membership around the world. Our approach as an open platform positions us to shape the future direction of water and sanitation, and I encourage you all, as members, to take advantage to the full of the opportunities this presents.
