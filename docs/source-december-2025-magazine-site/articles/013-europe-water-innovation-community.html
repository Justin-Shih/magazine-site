中文全文翻譯

**以合作回應歐洲水挑戰** Michelle H. Williams說明，知識與創新社群(KICs, Knowledge and Innovation Communities)如何形塑整體性的水管理方法。 歐洲正面對許多與氣候變遷及環境退化有關、複雜且相互連結的問題。若要實現永續、競爭力與韌性目標，必須增加對創新、企業創設及公共基礎設施的投資，並縮小技能落差（Draghi，2024）。 水務版圖廣大且零碎，迫切需要以更整體的方法看待水這項珍貴、有限且不可或缺的資源。海洋與淡水生態系正受氣候變遷和污染威脅，進而危及生物多樣性、經濟及飲用水品質。 未能掌握水回收及再利用機會，使這些挑戰更加嚴重；水資源稀缺增加，將導致糧食短缺與水不安全。同時，氣候相關極端天氣事件發生頻率提高，需要投資基礎設施，以預測並回應洪水與乾旱。 零碎的水、海洋與海事版圖阻礙創新進入市場，但可透過培養連結良好的跨部門生態系來扭轉，協助填補資金機會缺口並引導構想擴大。可規模化的創新，在於降低歐洲關鍵部門的水足跡，並開啟循環機會，為所有歐盟水體急需的生態狀態與健康帶來助益。把生態系改善與社會、經濟影響結合，仍是水務領域尚未充分開發的潛力。隨著提升歐洲適應氣候變遷與極端天氣能力的需求增加，這種跨部門作法將愈發重要。 然而，達成這些目標有賴於讓水、海洋及海事專業人員能面向未來，透過新技術訓練和聚焦創新及創業精神的教育，提升其能力。 當歐洲培養創新解決方案時，重要的是讓它們能成功從實驗室轉移至市場；也必須擅長擴大新創企業規模，並展示新技術和解決方案的價值，以確保水務部門邁向數位與智慧未來。水對日常生活、歐洲產業及經濟都很重要。歐盟及其成員國必須透過投資創新、教育與企業創設，在這些領域引領全球發展。 自2010年起，歐洲創新與技術研究院(EIT, European Institute for Innovation and Technology)投資建立跨越「知識三角」(Knowledge Triangle)的創新生態系；知識三角是教育、創新與企業創設之間的交會點。其目標是克服水務領域所面對的社會挑戰，也藉由擴大歐洲各地創新構想來增進歐洲競爭力。 EIT透過成立知識與創新社群(KICs)，投資歐洲教育、創新與企業創設的未來。透過整合知識三角並高度聚焦創業及企業創設，既有九個KIC已在能源、氣候、製造、食品及健康等多元領域取得重要成果。 KIC高度聚焦於社會挑戰的影響成果，特別是以人才發展、新企業創設及創新推出為基礎的產業影響。全歐洲跨部門的夥伴組織與專家共同辨識缺口並創造影響。其中一項核心工作是培訓企業家，協助他們把創新解決方案從研究帶到市場、吸引投資以擴大並展示創新價值。同時，既有部門組織可取得新人才、解決方案供應者、最新發展資訊、創新專案、員工升級或再培訓機會，以及強大的國際網絡。 從組織角度看，KIC把區域及國家專長放大到歐洲脈絡。KIC也希望克服零碎化，促進跨越傳統界線的合作，包括跨部門及跨學科合作。每一個KIC設有以區域為基礎的共同所在地中心(CLCs, Co-Location Centres)，作為跨國邊界的錨點，運用在地專業與創新生態系，並在歐洲架構中促進合作。這種區域錨定在需要額外支持讓創新解決方案起飛的國家尤其重要。其影響來自不同成員國夥伴組織的專業、關聯性及多樣性，也來自KIC採取真正歐洲視角的能力。KIC的額外價值還包括不同KIC主題間的綜效與合作，強化更廣泛夥伴關係，並為複雜社會挑戰提供高層級跨學科方法；這種跨部門、跨學科作法正是KIC的強項。 水已被確認為迫切的社會挑戰。因此，為了回應歐洲面對的挑戰並確保智慧水未來，EIT在其2021至2027年策略下，於2025年針對水、海洋及海事(WMM, Water, Marine and Maritime)部門與生態系的KIC發出公開徵件。到2025年底，將宣布新的Water KIC。透過EIT KIC的獨特模式，將建立全歐洲的水務社群，匯集企業、研究機構與大學，為WMM部門的未來進行教育、創新及投資。為使努力與影響最大化，KIC將與既有歐洲倡議及夥伴關係合作，建立跨主題、地域及組織類型的整體且包容生態系。 共有五個聯盟參與2025年未來Water KIC公開徵件。其中一個競標聯盟稱為「Allwaters」，名稱反映其提案的廣泛範圍與多元夥伴關係。Allwaters橫跨歐洲大陸，並將從北極連結到全球南方的水務版圖。它納入水、海洋及海事部門的前沿專長，涵蓋大學、研究組織、非政府組織、基金會、產業群聚、港口與公共機構等不同組織，也包括中小型及大型企業。Allwaters以整體觀點，將各部門視為複雜系統中相互連結的組成部分，目標是針對天生跨領域的挑戰，提供可移轉的解決方案與系統性創新。 Allwaters的核心價值，在於以包容、協作及開放方式確保活動具有關聯性與影響力。早期KIC反覆面臨的挑戰是起步、讓所有夥伴對齊及擴大夥伴關係。為克服這些挑戰，Allwaters夥伴積極參與設計及撰寫競標案，使其廣大參與夥伴網絡取得實質承諾及投資。有了這些支持，Allwaters已奠定可立即啟動的KIC基礎，策略是在KIC生命早期即達成財務永續。 Allwaters希望運用網絡力量，交付突破性創新、催生成功且具影響力的企業，並協助專業人員準備面對未來挑戰。其企圖是解放水、海洋及海事部門的潛能，運用影響力促進歐洲安全、韌性、永續性與競爭力。透過強化創新、投資教育及對產業的協調貢獻，目標是讓歐洲成為水務部門與藍色經濟的全球領導者。

Williams explains how Knowledge and Innovative Communities can forge a holistic approach to water management. urope is faced with a myriad of complex and interconnected issues linked to climate change and environmental degradation. To deliver on sustainability, competitiveness and resilience targets, there needs to be increased investment in innovation, business creation and public infrastructure, and a closing of the skills gap (Draghi, 2024). The water landscape is broad and fragmented, and there is an urgent need to take a more holistic approach to water as a valuable, finite and essential resource. Marine and freshwater ecosystems are threatened by climate change and pollution, which pose risks to biodiversity, the economy and our drinking water quality. These challenges are exacerbated by failures to seize opportunities to recycle and reuse water, with increased water scarcity that will lead to food shortages and water insecurity. At the same time, the increased incidence of extreme climate-related weather events requires investment in infrastructure for predicting and responding to flood and drought events. A fragmented water, marine and maritime landscape hinders the market deployment of innovations, but this can be turned around by fostering a well-connected, cross- sectoral ecosystem to assist in bridging the gaps in funding opportunities, and to guide the scaling of ideas. Scalable innovation lies in reducing the water footprint of key sectors in Europe and unlocking circular opportunities that can provide a much-needed boost to the ecological status and health of all EU waters. Combining ecosystem improvement with social and economic impact remains one of the untapped potentials of the water sector. This cross-sectoral approach will become ever more important as the need to enhance ~> Europe’s ability to adapt to climate change and extreme weather events increases. Achieving these goals relies, however, on future-proofing water, marine and maritime professionals through training in novel technologies and education in the field, focused on innovation and entrepreneurship. As European innovative solutions are fostered, it is important that they are successfully transferred from the laboratory to the market, and there is a need to excel in scaling startups and demonstrating the value of new technologies and solutions to secure a digital and smart future for the water sector. Water is important for our everyday lives, and for European industries and the economy. The EU and its member states must lead global development within these fields through investment in innovation, education and business creation. Since 2010, the European Institute for Innovation and Technology (EIT) has invested in the establishment of innovative ecosystems spanning the Knowledge Triangle: the nexus between education, innovation and business creation. Their ambition is to overcome societal challenges, like the ones faced by the water sector, but also to bolster European competitiveness by scaling innovative ideas found across the continent. EIT invests in the future of European education, innovation and business creation through the establishment of Knowledge and Innovation Communities (KICs). Through the integration of the Knowledge Triangle, and a strong focus on entrepreneurship and business creation, they have delivered significant results across the nine existing KICs in areas as diverse as energy, climate, manufacturing, food and health. fo) S iS S i > ic] & J eu Ss g =~ S s a (2) The KICs have a strong focus on delivering impact within societal challenges, focused especially on industrial impacts, based on talent development, creating new businesses and launching innovations. Partner organisations and experts across sectors throughout Europe come together to identify gaps and deliver impact. One of the core activities is the training of entrepreneurs to help them transfer innovative solutions from research to market, and to attract investment to scale and demonstrate the value of their innovations. Meanwhile, existing organisations within the sector gain access to new talent, solutions providers, information on the latest developments, innovation projects, the up- or re-skilling of their workers, and a powerful international network. From an organisational perspective, the KICs amplify regional and national expertise into a European context. The KICs also aim to overcome fragmentation and foster collaboration across traditional divides, both between sectors and disciplines. This regional anchoring is specifically strengthened in countries where innovative solutions need an additional boost to take off. This impact has been achieved because of the expertise, relevance and diversity of the different member state partner organisations, and the ability of the KICs to take a truly European perspective. An added value of the KIC are synergies and collaboration across KIC themes, which strengthen broader partnerships and provide a high-level interdisciplinary approach to complex societal challenges. This cross-sectoral, cross-discipline approach is one of the strengths of the KICs. Water has been identified as an urgent societal challenge. Therefore, to address the challenges Europe faces and to secure a smart water future, EIT launched an open call for a KIC in the Water, Marine and Maritime (WMM) sectors and ecosystems in 2025, as part of its strategy for 2021-27. By the end of 2025, a new Water KIC will be announced. Through the unique EIT KIC model, the new European-wide water community will be established to bring together businesses, research institutions and universities to educate, innovate and invest in the future of the WMM sectors. To maximise effort and impact, the KIC will work together with existing European initiatives and partnerships to create a holistic and inclusive ecosystem that works across thematic areas, geography and types of organisations. Five consortia bid into the 2025 EIT open call for a future Water KIC. One of the bidding consortia is called ‘Allwaters’ because of the broad scope of its proposal and diverse partnership. Allwaters spans the European continent, but will connect the water landscape from the Arctic to the Global South. It includes cutting-edge expertise within the water, marine and maritime sectors, across different organisations, from universities and research organisations through to NGOs, foundations, industry clusters, ports and public institutions, including small and large enterprises. By ensuring a holistic view and approaching the sectors as interconnected components of a complex system, Allwaters intends to deliver transferable solutions and systemic innovations to challenges that are cross-cutting by nature. The core value of Allwaters is its inclusive, collaborative and open approach to ensuring that activities are relevant and impactful. A recurring challenge with earlier KICs has been in getting off the ground early, aligning all partners and growing the partnership. To overcome these challenges, the Allwaters partners have been actively involved in the design and writing of the Allwaters bid, which has led to significant commitment and investment being secured across its wide network of engaged partners. With this support, Allwaters has laid down the foundation for a launch-ready KIC, with the strategy to become financially sustainable early in the life of the KIC. Allwaters aims to use the power of the network to deliver groundbreaking innovation, launch successful and impactful businesses, and help professionals become ready for future challenges. The ambition is to unlock the potential of the water, marine and maritime sectors, and to leverage impact to contribute to Europe’s safety, resilience, sustainability and competitiveness. With strengthened innovation, investments in education and a coordinated contribution to the industry, the goal is to position Europe as a global leader in the water sector and the blue economy.
