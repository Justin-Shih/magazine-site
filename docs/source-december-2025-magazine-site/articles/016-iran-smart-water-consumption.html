中文全文翻譯

**伊朗推動智慧用水** Pezhman Taherei說明，面對氣候變遷與反覆乾旱，伊朗水務機關如何重新定義與用戶溝通的方式。 伊朗德黑蘭如今正式被指定為「水敏感城市」，已成為都市水管理的案例。2025年春季推出的「20×30＝600公升」行動，展現多階段、多媒體的公眾參與作法。行動的核心訊息簡單但有力：若每位市民每天至少節省20公升水，每人每月合計即可節省600公升。教育內容是此行動的核心，並以容易取得的PDF、資訊圖表、圖片及短影片等形式提供；同時搭配互動競賽，以獎品鼓勵民眾參與。 德黑蘭省供水與污水局執行長Mohsen Ardakani表示：「透過節水設備與簡單的行為改變，我們可將用水量降低多達30%。即使只減少20%，每年也可節省3.5億立方公尺，約相當於德黑蘭原水來源之一阿米爾卡比爾水壩(Amir Kabir Dam)容量的1.5倍。這不只是必要之舉，也是共同適應新氣候現實的承諾。」 **數位工具與遊戲化** 布什爾省(Bushehr Province)推出名為「Aabiane」的手機遊戲，採取創新作法，向5至11歲兒童教導節水行為。這款電玩模擬真實生活中的水管理情境；玩家扮演一般市民，在洗滌、清潔與園藝等日常工作中，做出用水決定。 遊戲機制奠基於行為科學與教育心理學，會以遊戲貨幣及城市建造機會，獎勵高效率用水。排行榜與進度報告可在學生間建立社群感及良性競爭，也讓家庭得到其共同影響的回饋。 布什爾省供水與污水公司執行長Ali Bardestani說：「我們理解到，若要永續地改變行為，必須從年輕時開始。『Aabiane』不只是遊戲，它是建立決策能力、提升水危機意識，以及在家庭和學校推動資源管理的工具。」 這兩項倡議都把數位工具與雙向溝通列為優先。透過線上平台和應用程式功能，鼓勵市民分享回饋、通報困難並提出改善建議。這種開放對話不僅建立公眾信任，也讓機關能即時調整策略。 初步結果令人鼓舞，顯示水資源意識提高、家庭用水量可量測地降低，且共同責任感正在成長。值得注意的是，這些行動成功接觸到傳統上不易參與的群體，例如兒童與青少年。 **可供全球複製的藍圖** 德黑蘭與布什爾的經驗顯示，結合傳統媒體、數位工具及遊戲化，可以有效驅動節水。透過聚焦青年參與，並培養回饋與協作文化，這些倡議為世界各地水務機關提供可複製的模式。 隨著氣候壓力升高，IWA公共與用戶溝通專業小組(Public and Customer Communications Specialist Group)鼓勵各地公用事業投資於具創意、包容及互動性的溝通策略，讓下一代成為堅定的水資源守護者。 **作者** Pezhman Taherei是IWA公共與用戶溝通專業小組管理委員會成員、IWA小型供水及污水系統專業小組管理委員會成員，並擔任伊朗IWA最高委員會理事。

Iran’s drive for smart _ water consumption Pezhman Taherei explains how in the face of climate change and recurring drougnts, Iranian water authorities are redefining how they communicate with customers. ehran, Iran, now officially designated a ‘water-sensitive city’, has become a case study in urban water management. The ‘20x30 = 600 Litres’ campaign, launched in spring 2025, exemplifies a multi-stage, multimedia approach to public engagement. The campaign’s core message is simple yet powerful: if every citizen saves at least 20 litres of water daily, collectively 600 litres of water would be saved per person each month. Educational content is at the heart of the campaign, delivered in accessible formats, such as PDFs, infographics, images, and short videos, alongside interactive competitions, incentivising participation with prizes. “By using water-saving devices and simple behavioural changes, we can reduce consumption by up to 30%,” says Mohsen Ardakani, CEO, Tehran Province Water and Sewerage Authority. “With only a 20% reduction, we can save 350 million cubic metres annually, equivalent to 1.5 times the capacity of Amir Kabir Dam, one of the raw water resources of Tehran. This is not just a necessity — it’s a collective commitment to adapt to our new climate reality.” Digital tools and gamification Bushehr Province has taken a pioneering step by launching ‘Aabiane’, a mobile game designed to teach water-saving behaviours to children aged 5-11. Players take on the role of everyday citizens, making daily decisions about water use in tasks like washing, cleaning, and gardening. The game’s mechanics are rooted in behavioural science and educational psychology, rewarding efficient water use with in-game currency and city-building opportunities. Leaderboards and progress reports foster a sense of community and healthy competition among students, while also providing families with feedback on their collective impact. Ali Bardestani, CEO, Bushehr Province Water and Wastewater Company, says: “We realised that to change behaviours sustainably, we must start young. ‘Aabiane’ is more than a game ~ it’s a tool for building decision-making skills, raising awareness about the water crisis, and promoting resource management at home and in schools.” Both of these initiatives prioritise digital tools and two-way communication. Citizens are encouraged to share feedback, report challenges, and suggest improvements via online platforms and in-app features. This open dialogue not only builds public trust but also enables authorities to refine their strategies in real-time. The results are promising, revealing increased awareness, measurable reductions in household water use, and a growing sense of shared responsibility. Notably, the campaigns have succeeded in reaching groups that are traditionally hard to engage, such as children and adolescents. A blueprint for global replication The experiences of Tehran and Bushehr illustrate the power of combining traditional media, digital tools, and gamification to drive water conservation. By focusing on youth engagement and fostering a culture of feedback and collaboration, these initiatives offer a replicable model for water authorities worldwide.
